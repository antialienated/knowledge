# EWM (Suing as mother and next friend) v VS (Children's Case 746 of 2019) [2024] KEMC 82 (KLR)

**Neutral citation:** [2024] KEMC 82 (KLR)  
**Court:** Nairobi Children's Court (Magistrate's Court, Family division), Republic of Kenya  
**Decided:** 2024-10-01  
**Panel:** JC Kibosia, Principal Magistrate (sitting alone)  

## Why this case matters

EWM v VS is a first-instance Kenyan children's-court judgment, published on Kenya Law, in which the court expressly refuses a custodial mother's prayer that the non-resident father be denied access on the ground that such an order 'amounts to parental alienation' and is inconsistent with the equal parental responsibility guaranteed by Article 53 of the Constitution of Kenya 2010. The case arose from an alleged wrongful retention of the child in the United States after an agreed medical trip; the court nonetheless preserved the mother's primary care under the tender-years line (H G G v Y P; Sospeter Ojaamong v Lynette Amondi Otieno) and the Supreme Court's MAK v RMAA guidelines, granted joint legal custody, and imposed a structured audio-visual and in-person access regime for the father together with exit-restriction and passport-deposit orders. It matters because it is a rare published African decision that uses the term 'parental alienation' in its operative reasoning — as a shorthand for the impermissibility of excluding one parent, not as a clinical diagnosis and without any expert evidence — and it does so at magistrate level, which shows how the vocabulary has entered routine Kenyan practice under the Children Act 2022.

## Procedural history

Children's Case 746 of 2019 in the Nairobi Children's Court. The Plaintiff mother sued for (a) a prayer recorded as 'spent', (b) a permanent order restraining the Defendant from leaving the Court's jurisdiction with the minor, (c) actual custody, care and control, (d) a wardship order and (e) costs (para 1). Her case was that in early 2019 the minor required urgent medical attention, that both parties agreed the procedure be carried out in the United States, that she consented to the travel, that the Defendant and the minor travelled and that the minor did not come back as agreed, the Defendant having switched off his phone; she therefore came to Court for intervention/return orders (para 2). The Defendant's case was that when the child was examined in the United States he was found to have other complications requiring urgent medical intervention, of which he informed the Plaintiff (para 3). An issue of contempt of Court by the Plaintiff arose during the proceedings; the Court declined to pursue it (para 11). Judgment was dated, signed and delivered virtually on 1 October 2024 in the presence of Ms. Nganga for the Plaintiff and Mr. Wahome (holding brief for Ms. Njuguna) for the Defendant. Kenya Law published the judgment on 10 April 2025 with the outcome recorded as 'Allowed in part'.

## Holding

(1) The paramountcy of the child's best interests under Article 53(2) of the Constitution and section 8 of the Children Act 2022, read with the section 103 custody factors and the Supreme Court's eleven MAK v RMAA guidelines (including guideline 8: the need to preserve personal relations and direct contact with both parents unless not in the child's best interests, in which case supervised access must be granted), governs custody and access. (2) The mother has been the primary parent and the status quo should not be disturbed; the child has already suffered psychological harm from the alleged abduction and instability. (3) The mother's prayer that the father be denied access 'is not tenable since it amounts to parental alienation'; Article 53 'speaks to equal parental rights', so the father's participation must be structured, not excluded. (4) Financial responsibility for the child cannot be one-sided where the other parent is actively involved in the child's life. (5) Contempt proceedings against the mother would aggravate the rift and are not pursued, in the child's best interest.

## Outcome

Claim allowed in part. Joint legal custody to both parents; actual custody, care and control to the Plaintiff/mother (wardship order unnecessary); Defendant/father granted access — while in the United States, unlimited but reasonable audio-visual contact (EAT); while in Kenya, alternate weekends (Saturday 9am to Sunday 5pm, pick-up/drop-off at the nearest recreational facility) and half of school holidays alternating Easter and Christmas. Defendant to invest in an education policy of his choice at KES 10,000 per month within 30 days as his contribution to the minor's educational needs. Neither party to leave the Court's jurisdiction with the minor without a court order; the minor's passport(s) to be deposited in Court and the immigration department notified. No order as to costs (family matter); parties at liberty to apply.

## Comparative jurisprudence

- **MAK v RMAA & 4 others (Petition 2 (E003) of 2022) [2023] KESC 21 (KLR)** (KE) — Supreme Court of Kenya guidelines for balancing a child's best interests against parental rights and responsibility; quoted in full at para 7 and applied here, in particular guideline 8 on preserving direct contact with both parents.
- **H G G v Y P [2017] eKLR** (KE) — High Court statement of the tender-years principle and of the exceptional circumstances required to deny a mother custody; quoted at para 8 as the basis for preserving the mother's primary care.
- **Sospeter Ojaamong v Lynette Amondi Otieno, Civil Appeal 176 of 2006** (KE) — Court of Appeal authority (citing Martha Olela & Another v Jackson Obiera, Civil Application No. Nairobi 16 of 1979) that custody of children of tender years goes to the mother absent special and peculiar disqualifying circumstances; quoted at para 9.
- **T.L.D v B.G (015642/2022) [2023] ZAGPJHC 801** (ZA) — [`tld-v-bg-2023-zagpjhc-801-south-africa`](./tld-v-bg-2023-zagpjhc-801-south-africa.md) — The other sub-Saharan African decision in this knowledge base; South African High Court engagement with parental-alienation framing at greater length and with expert evidence, against which the summary Kenyan magistrate-level use of the term can be read.
- **Vivek Singh v Romani Singh, (2017) 3 SCC 231** (IN) — [`vivek-singh-v-romani-singh-2017-india`](./vivek-singh-v-romani-singh-2017-india.md) — Common-law apex decision in the Global South treating exclusion of a parent as harmful to the child within a best-interests framework; comparable structural reasoning (equal parental responsibility, contact as the child's entitlement) without a diagnostic PA construct.

## Editorial notes

- PRIMARY SOURCE: every fact, quotation and order in this entry was verified against the full text of the judgment as published on Kenya Law (new.kenyalaw.org) on 2026-09-23. Kenya Law's metadata records: court 'Magistrate's Court', court station 'Nairobi Children's Court', court division 'Family', judge 'JC Kibosia', outcome 'Allowed in part', date published 10 April 2025.
- LEVEL OF COURT: this is a magistrate's-court (Nairobi Children's Court) judgment, not an appellate or apex decision. It is included because it is the Kenyan decision on Kenya Law that expressly reasons with the term 'parental alienation'; it has no binding precedential force beyond the case.
- USE OF THE TERM: 'parental alienation' appears once (para 9) as the court's characterisation of the mother's prayer that the father be denied all access. No psychologist, social-inquiry report or other expert evidence on alienation is referred to; the court does not treat alienation as a diagnosis or syndrome. The word 'abduction' is placed in quotation marks by the court and prefaced with 'alleged'.
- NAMED-PERSON PROTOCOL: the parties appear as 'EWM' and 'VS' exactly as published by Kenya Law; the child is not named and is described only as 'the minor'. Counsel (Ms. Nganga; Mr. Wahome holding brief for Ms. Njuguna) and the magistrate are named as in the judgment. The judgment's attendance line also records 'Mr. Mohammed CA' (court assistant); not reproduced in the counsel array as he is not counsel.
- SIC: para 3 of the published text reads 'In his evidence, he reiterated that he did abduct the child'. Read with paras 2-3 and 9 ('alleged "abduction"'), this appears to be a typographical omission in the published judgment (likely 'did not'); it is reproduced here as published and not corrected.
- NOT STATED IN PRIMARY TEXT: the child's year of birth (only 'the minor is 10 years old' at para 9), the dates of the Defendant's departure with the child beyond 'early 2019', the specific medical condition, and any first-instance interlocutory orders made between 2019 and 2024.
- 'EAT' in Final Order (iii) is reproduced as published; in context it denotes East Africa Time for the audio-visual contact schedule.
- The judgment cites an online article (Moges and Weber, 2014) on inter-parental conflict at para 13; the citation is the court's own and has not been independently verified for this entry.
