# CEE cooperation-duty + contact-framework comparative — PL, CZ, HU, RO, SI, HR

**Jurisdiction:** Comparative · **Coverage:** Poland · Czech Republic · Hungary · Romania · Slovenia · Croatia

A side-by-side analytical comparison of the six Central and Eastern European cooperation-duty + contact frameworks — the post-socialist family codes that recodified parental responsibility and contact regulation after 1990. The CEE jurisdictions converge on a *welfare-of-the-child* + *joint-exercise* + *cooperation-duty* framework, but diverge significantly on facilitation-duty articulation, enforcement mechanism, and PA-pattern responsiveness.

## Four-tier classification by facilitation-duty articulation strength

### Tier 1 — Express positive-action duty (most explicit)

**Slovenia (DZ art. 143 (2))** — *prizadevati si mora* (must strive). The Slovenian framework expressly codifies a *positive-effort* duty: the resident parent must not merely refrain from obstruction but must *actively strive* for the child's *appropriate relationship toward contact* with the other parent. This is doctrinally distinctive — directly addresses the alienation-pattern psychological dynamic (the child's relationship toward contact, not just contact itself).

**Croatia (OZ art. 117 (2))** — *dužan je omogućiti* (obliged to enable). The Croatian framework codifies a *positive-enable* duty alongside the negative-refrain duty. The dual-prong structure operates similarly to Slovenia's framework. The reciprocal duty at art. 117 (3) on the contact-exercising parent closes the structural gap.

### Tier 2 — Express dual right-and-obligation framing

**Poland (KRO art. 113 § 1)** — *prawo i obowiązek* (right and obligation). The 2009 nowelizacja's right-and-obligation framing closes the structural gap where the targeted parent could be passive in the face of alienation. The non-resident parent has both a right *and* an obligation to maintain contact — passivity is not lawful.

**Hungary (Ptk § 4:178 (2))** — *jogosult és köteles* (entitled and obliged) + *zavartalan kapcsolattartást biztosítani* (ensure undisturbed contact). The Hungarian framework combines the right-and-obligation framing with an *ensure undisturbed contact* duty on the resident parent — operationally close to the Slovenian/Croatian positive duties.

**Romania (Codul civil art. 487)** — *dreptul și îndatorirea* (right and obligation). The Romanian framing operates at the parental-authority content level (art. 487) and through the post-divorce *în comun* (joint) default (art. 397).

### Tier 3 — Express mutual-respect duty

**Czech Republic (NOZ § 891 (1))** — *respektovat se navzájem* (respect each other) + *umožnit styk* (enable contact). The Czech framework expressly codifies mutual respect alongside the enable-contact duty. The *respektovat* framing provides structural foundation for PA-pattern findings where the alienating parent's conduct demonstrates disrespect for the targeted parent's role.

## Doctrinal analysis — five key dimensions

### 1. Right + obligation framing

| Jurisdiction | Framing | Source provision |
|---|---|---|
| Poland | *prawo i obowiązek* | KRO art. 113 § 1 |
| Hungary | *jogosult és köteles* | Ptk § 4:178 (2) |
| Romania | *dreptul și îndatorirea* | Codul civil art. 487 |
| Slovenia | Implicit via art. 143 (2) duties | DZ art. 143 (2) |
| Croatia | *dužni i imaju pravo* (duty first) | OZ art. 95 (1) |
| Czech Republic | Implicit via § 865 (1) | NOZ § 865 (1) |

Poland, Hungary, Romania, and Croatia have express right + obligation framing in their statutes. Croatia is distinctive for placing *dužni* (duty) before *pravo* (right) — doctrinally prioritising parental obligation. Slovenia and Czech Republic rely on the duty-content of the framework provisions to achieve equivalent operational effect.
