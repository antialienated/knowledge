# Serbia Porodični zakon arts. 60 · 67 · 68 · 71 · 76 · 77 · 78 — Roditeljsko pravo and lični odnosi (Verbatim)

# Serbia Porodični zakon (Family Act 2005) — Roditeljsko pravo i lični odnosi (Verbatim)

## TL;DR

Serbia's **Porodični zakon** (Family Act, Official Gazette RS No. 18/2005, in force 1 July 2005, subsequently amended 72/2011, 6/2015, 60/2024) governs family law as a self-contained statute. Chapter III (**Roditeljsko pravo** — Parental Right), arts. 59 – 82, sets the substance of parental authority and the child-parent contact regime. Four provisions carry the operational anti-alienation weight: **art. 61(2)** (child's right to maintain personal relations with the parent with whom the child does not live); **art. 67** (joint exercise of parental right by mutual agreement — sporazumno); **art. 68** (sole exercise only where parents do not live together and by agreement or court decision, not by unilateral act); **art. 78** (parent who does not exercise parental right retains the right to maintain personal relations with the child, and the right to decide jointly on questions substantially affecting the child's life). The Serbian text below is drawn from the consolidated Paragraf/Official Gazette publication; the English is a working translation for research use. The Serbian original controls.

## Statute — Verbatim Text

### Art. 59 PZ — Nosioci roditeljskog prava (Bearers of the parental right)

**Serbian (consolidated text):**

> (1) Roditeljsko pravo pripada zajednički majci i ocu.
>
> (2) Roditelji vrše roditeljsko pravo zajednički i sporazumno kada vode zajednički život.
>
> (3) Roditelji su ravnopravni u vršenju roditeljskog prava.

**English (working translation):**

> (1) Parental right belongs jointly to the mother and the father.
>
> (2) The parents exercise parental right jointly and by mutual agreement when they lead a common life.
>
> (3) The parents are equal in the exercise of parental right.

**Operational note:** Art. 59(3) codifies the equality principle at the level of doctrine — parents are *ravnopravni* in the exercise of roditeljsko pravo. The starting point is joint bearing (paragraph 1), joint exercise by agreement (paragraph 2), and formal equality of standing (paragraph 3). Any divergence from joint exercise must be justified under arts. 67 – 68.

### Art. 60 PZ — Sadržina roditeljskog prava (Content of the parental right)

**Serbian:**

> (1) Roditeljsko pravo je izvedeno iz dužnosti roditelja i postoji samo u meri koja je potrebna za zaštitu ličnosti, prava i interesa deteta.
>
> (2) Sadržinu roditeljskog prava čine dužnosti i prava roditelja: da se staraju o detetu; da čuvaju i podižu dete; da vaspitavaju dete; da obrazuju dete; da zastupaju dete; da izdržavaju dete; i da upravljaju i raspolažu imovinom deteta.

**English:**

> (1) Parental right is derived from the duty of the parents and exists only to the extent necessary for the protection of the personality, rights and interests of the child.
>
> (2) The content of the parental right consists of the duties and rights of the parents: to care for the child; to keep and raise the child; to educate the child; to give education to the child; to represent the child; to maintain the child; and to manage and dispose of the child's property.

**Operational note:** Art. 60(1) is textually distinctive: roditeljsko pravo is *derived from duty* — the right exists as an instrument for the discharge of duty and only to the extent necessary for the child's protection. This "duty-derived right" framing has a specific operational consequence: exercises of the right that are *not* in service of duty (e.g. exercise of aizgadniba-style representation powers to obstruct the other parent) fall outside the statutory grant of the right and are *ultra vires*. This is a more textually explicit framing than Estonia's or Latvia's abuse-of-right clauses.

### Art. 61 PZ — Prava deteta (Rights of the child)

**Serbian (extracts, in the 2005 baseline consolidated with subsequent amendments):**

> (1) Dete ima pravo da živi sa roditeljima.
>
> (2) Dete koje ne živi sa oba roditelja ima pravo da održava lične odnose sa roditeljem sa kojim ne živi.
>
> (3) Pravo deteta iz stava 2. ovog člana može biti ograničeno samo sudskom odlukom kada je to u najboljem interesu deteta.
>
> (4) Sud može doneti odluku o ograničavanju prava deteta iz stava 2. ovog člana ako postoji opasnost za život, zdravlje ili psihofizički razvoj deteta.

**English:**

> (1) The child has the right to live with the parents.
>
> (2) A child who does not live with both parents shall have the right to maintain personal relations with the parent with whom the child does not live.
>
> (3) The right of the child referred to in paragraph 2 of this Article may be limited only by a court decision where this is in the best interests of the child.
>
> (4) A court may render a decision on the limitation of the right of the child referred to in paragraph 2 of this Article where there is a danger to the life, health or psychophysical development of the child.

**Operational note:** Art. 61(2) frames the contact right as **a right of the child**, not of the parent — the same architectural choice made in Estonia § 143, Latvia art. 181, Lithuania § 3.170(2) and Ukraine art. 153. Art. 61(3) then restricts the *limitation* power: only a court decision, only where in the best interests, and (paragraph 4) only where there is a danger to life, health or psychophysical development. In pleading terms this makes any *de facto* limitation imposed by the residential parent an act *outside* the statutory limitation regime and enforceable directly under arts. 68 and 78.
