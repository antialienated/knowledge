# ABGB § 181 — Entziehung oder Einschränkung der Obsorge (verbatim, Absatz 1)

# ABGB § 181 — Entziehung oder Einschränkung der Obsorge (verbatim, Absatz 1)

**Jurisdiction:** Austria · **Citation:** *Allgemeines Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch* § 181 · **Authoritative source:** [ris.bka.gv.at — § 181 ABGB](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/NormDokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Gesetzesnummer=10001622&Paragraf=181)

The **apex-tier remedy** in Austrian family law against parental misconduct. ABGB § 181 establishes the court's power to entirely or partially withdraw *Obsorge* (parental responsibility) where the parents' conduct endangers the welfare of the minor child. It is the Austrian structural parallel to:
- [Italy Codice Civile art. 330 *decadenza*](../italy/codice-civile-art-330-decadenza-responsabilita-genitoriale.md)
- [Germany BGB § 1666 Kindeswohlgefährdung remedies](../germany/bgb-1666-kindeswohlgefahrdung-verbatim.md)
- [UK Children Act 1989 s. 31 care and supervision orders](../united-kingdom/children-act-1989-section-31-care-and-supervision-orders.md)

§ 181 ABGB is the Austrian apex-tier provision invoked in the most severe sustained alienating-conduct cases — see [graduated-remedy ladder comparative](../comparative/graduated-remedy-ladder-it-de-at-uk.md). It operates within the broader Austrian framework that includes [§ 159 Wohlverhaltensgebot + § 187 Kontaktrecht](abgb-159-187-wohlverhaltensgebot-kontaktrecht-verbatim.md) and the graduated remedies under §§ 177-181.

## Verbatim text (current consolidated)

### § 181 Absatz 1 ABGB — Entziehung oder Einschränkung der Obsorge

> Gefährden die Eltern durch ihr Verhalten das Wohl des minderjährigen Kindes, so hat das Gericht, von wem immer es angerufen wird, die zur Sicherung des Wohles des Kindes nötigen Verfügungen zu treffen. Besonders darf das Gericht die Obsorge für das Kind ganz oder teilweise, auch gesetzlich vorgesehene Einwilligungs- und Zustimmungsrechte, entziehen. Im Einzelfall kann das Gericht auch eine gesetzlich erforderliche Einwilligung oder Zustimmung ersetzen, wenn keine gerechtfertigten Gründe für die Weigerung vorliegen.

*Paragraphs 2-4 set out the procedural and consequential framework — standing for application (the other parent, relatives, etc.); the scope of withdrawal extending to gesetzliche Vertretung; and the conditions for ersatz-Einwilligung.*

## English working translation

### § 181 paragraph 1 ABGB — Withdrawal or restriction of Obsorge

> Where the parents endanger the welfare of the minor child through their conduct, the court — by whomever it is invoked — must take the measures necessary to safeguard the welfare of the child. In particular, the court may withdraw Obsorge for the child entirely or partially, including the legally provided rights of consent and approval. In an individual case, the court may also substitute a legally required consent or approval where no justified grounds for refusal exist.

## Why this matters for PA

- **Apex-tier framework.** § 181 ABGB is the Austrian apex-tier remedy — operative where the parents' conduct (or in PA cases, the alienating parent's conduct) endangers the welfare of the minor child. The trigger is *Gefährden* + *durch ihr Verhalten* + *das Wohl des minderjährigen Kindes* — conduct-based welfare endangerment.
- **Three-step remedy structure within § 181 (1).** The provision authorises:
  - General measures (*die zur Sicherung des Wohles des Kindes nötigen Verfügungen*) — open-ended welfare-protective measures
  - Entire or partial withdrawal of Obsorge — the principal apex-tier remedy
  - Substitute consent (*ersatzweise Einwilligung*) — court substitution for parental consent in single-decision contexts where no justified grounds for refusal exist
- **The "von wem immer es angerufen wird" clause.** *By whomever it is invoked.* The court has jurisdiction regardless of who triggered the proceedings — Pubblico-Ministero-equivalent procedural openness. In Austrian practice this means the *Kinder- und Jugendhilfe* (youth welfare authority), the *Pubblico Ministero* equivalent, or any concerned party can invoke § 181.
- **Use in sustained PA cases.** The Austrian Oberster Gerichtshof has applied § 181 in extreme sustained PA-pattern cases where:
  - The alienating parent's conduct has been established as welfare-endangering
  - Less-invasive remedies under [§ 187 (2) Kontaktrecht restriction](abgb-159-187-wohlverhaltensgebot-kontaktrecht-verbatim.md) have been exhausted or shown demonstrably inadequate
  - The welfare assessment supports Obsorge transfer to the targeted parent or removal entirely
- **Doctrinal significance — partial vs entire withdrawal.** *Ganz oder teilweise* — entirely or partially. The court has substantial discretion to fashion the remedy proportionate to the conduct. In PA cases this typically operates as:
  - Partial withdrawal — removing specific decision-making rights (e.g. school choice, medical decisions) from the alienating parent
  - Entire withdrawal — full Obsorge transfer to the targeted parent
- **The Verhaltens-based trigger.** The provision focuses on *Verhalten* (conduct) — not on diagnostic categorisation. This is doctrinally consistent with the Austrian functional-recognition approach to PA (see [PA recognition-status comparative](../comparative/pa-recognition-status-comparative.md)) — Austria does not require SAP/PAS syndromal diagnosis; conduct-pattern findings are sufficient.

## Procedural application

- The competent court is the *Bezirksgericht* (district court) sitting as *Pflegschaftsgericht*; proceedings follow the [*Außerstreitgesetz* (AußStrG)](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/) non-contentious framework
- *Familiengerichtshilfe* (family-court support service) provides multidisciplinary welfare assessment
- The *Kinder- und Jugendhilfe* (youth welfare authority) has standing to apply
- Where Obsorge is withdrawn, transfer typically goes to the other parent; in extreme cases to third-party carer or guardianship
