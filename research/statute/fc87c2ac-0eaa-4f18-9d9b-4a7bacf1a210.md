# Indonesia UU 1/1974 Perkawinan arts. 41 · 45 · 47 · 49 and UU 23/2002 Perlindungan Anak arts. 14 · 26 (Verbatim)

# Indonesia UU 1/1974 Perkawinan and UU 23/2002 Perlindungan Anak (Verbatim)

## TL;DR

Indonesia governs family law on a **dual-track** basis. The civil track runs under **Undang-Undang Nomor 1 Tahun 1974 tentang Perkawinan** (Law 1/1974 on Marriage, in force 2 January 1974 with implementing regulation of 1 October 1975), as amended by **UU 16/2019** in respect of marriage age. Child protection runs under **Undang-Undang Nomor 23 Tahun 2002 tentang Perlindungan Anak** (Law 23/2002 on Child Protection), as amended by **UU 35/2014** and **UU 17/2016**. For Muslim families the substantive framework additionally runs under the **Kompilasi Hukum Islam** (Compilation of Islamic Law, Presidential Instruction No. 1 of 1991, given force by Peraturan Pemerintah), administered by the Peradilan Agama (Religious Courts) which have exclusive jurisdiction over Islamic-family matters under **UU 3/2006** amending **UU 7/1989 tentang Peradilan Agama**. Four provisions carry the operational anti-alienation weight in the civil track: **UU 1/1974 art. 41(a)** (parents' duty to raise, care for and educate the child continues after divorce, in the child's interest); **UU 1/1974 art. 45** (parents' equal duty to educate the child until majority or marriage); **UU 23/2002 art. 14(1)** (the child's right not to be separated from his or her parents against his or her will, except where separation is necessary in the best interests of the child); **UU 23/2002 art. 26(1)(c)** (parents' duty to prevent marriage before the child reaches majority). The Indonesian text below is drawn from the consolidated JDIH publication; the English is a working translation for research use. Indonesia is *not* a party to the Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction (1980) — a defining constraint on cross-border civil-track and Islamic-track alienation practice, with substantial cross-border case-flow to Malaysia, Singapore, the Netherlands, Australia, Saudi Arabia and Japan.

## Statute — Verbatim Text

### UU 1/1974 art. 41 — Legal Consequences of Divorce for Parental Duties

**Indonesian (consolidated text):**

> Akibat putusnya perkawinan karena perceraian ialah:
>
> a. Baik ibu atau bapak tetap berkewajiban memelihara dan mendidik anak-anaknya, semata-mata berdasarkan kepentingan anak, bilamana ada perselisihan mengenai penguasaan anak-anak, Pengadilan memberi keputusannya;
>
> b. Bapak yang bertanggung jawab atas semua biaya pemeliharaan dan pendidikan yang diperlukan anak itu; bilamana bapak dalam kenyataan tidak dapat memenuhi kewajiban tersebut, Pengadilan dapat menentukan bahwa ibu ikut memikul biaya tersebut;
>
> c. Pengadilan dapat mewajibkan kepada bekas suami untuk memberikan biaya penghidupan dan/atau menentukan sesuatu kewajiban bagi bekas isteri.

**English (working translation):**

> The consequences of the dissolution of marriage by divorce shall be:
>
> (a) Both the mother and the father shall remain under the duty to maintain and educate their children, solely on the basis of the interest of the children; where there is a dispute concerning the *penguasaan* (holding of authority over) the children, the Court shall issue its decision;
>
> (b) The father shall be responsible for all the costs of maintenance and education necessary for the child; where in fact the father is unable to fulfil that duty, the Court may determine that the mother shall also bear that cost;
>
> (c) The Court may require the former husband to provide a subsistence allowance and/or determine an obligation on the former wife.

**Operational note:** Art. 41(a) is the **operational anti-alienation core** of the Indonesian civil track. Three features:

  - **Duty preservation after divorce.** "**Both the mother and the father shall remain**" (baik ibu atau bapak tetap berkewajiban) — the duty of care and education is preserved on both parents *by force of statute*, regardless of the divorce judgment or the *penguasaan* designation. This is the doctrinal footing for the argument that obstruction of the other parent's role is a failure of the *residential* parent's own duty under art. 41(a), not merely a violation of the other parent's rights.
  - **"Solely on the basis of the interest of the child" (semata-mata berdasarkan kepentingan anak).** The best-interests standard governs both parents' exercise of the preserved duty. Exercises inconsistent with the child's interest — including obstruction that harms the child's development — are outside the statutory grant.
  - **Court-referral route on disputes over penguasaan.** The Court decides where the parents cannot agree. "Penguasaan" is a broader concept than English-common-law "custody" — it includes decision-making authority, physical care and representation. The Court's determination fixes all three.

### UU 1/1974 art. 45 — Parents' Duty to Raise and Educate the Child

**Indonesian:**

> (1) Kedua orang tua wajib memelihara dan mendidik anak-anak mereka sebaik-baiknya.
>
> (2) Kewajiban orang tua yang dimaksud dalam ayat (1) pasal ini berlaku sampai anak itu kawin atau dapat berdiri sendiri, kewajiban mana berlaku terus meskipun perkawinan antara kedua orang tua putus.

**English:**

> (1) Both parents shall be obliged to maintain and educate their children in the best possible manner.
>
> (2) The duty of the parents referred to in paragraph (1) of this article shall apply until the child marries or is able to stand alone; that duty shall continue even where the marriage between the two parents has been dissolved.

**Operational note:** Art. 45(1) codifies the parents' *joint* duty to maintain and educate. Art. 45(2) is the *duration and preservation* clause — the duty continues until the child marries or can stand alone (an intriguingly wide concept incorporating economic independence) and *survives divorce*. The duty is not extinguishable by court order or by agreement; it is a statutory continuing obligation.
