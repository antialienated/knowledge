# Cooperation-duty statutory map — Nordic + DACH comparative (Wohlverhaltensgebot family)

**Jurisdictions covered:** Germany · Austria · Norway · Sweden · Iceland · *(plus Denmark, Finland on the descriptive layer)*

A comparative-law map of the **affirmative parental cooperation duty** (often called the *Wohlverhaltensgebot* in DACH jurisdictions, *yhteistyövelvoite* in Finland, joint-responsibility clauses in Nordic codes) — the statutory obligation imposed on separated parents to refrain from conduct that impairs the child's relationship with the other parent. This is the principal statutory hook on which European *Entfremdung* / alienation jurisprudence is built, parallel to but distinct from PA syndrome diagnostic frameworks.

## The cooperation-duty clause across jurisdictions

| Jurisdiction | Statute | Operative duty clause (verbatim, original language) |
|---|---|---|
| **Germany** | BGB § 1684 (2) | *Die Eltern haben alles zu unterlassen, was das Verhältnis des Kindes zum jeweils anderen Elternteil beeinträchtigt oder die Erziehung erschwert.* |
| **Austria** | ABGB § 159 | *Bei Ausübung der Rechte und Erfüllung der Pflichten nach diesem Hauptstück ist zur Wahrung des Kindeswohls alles zu unterlassen, was das Verhältnis des Minderjährigen zu anderen Personen, denen nach diesem Hauptstück das Kind betreffende Rechte und Pflichten zukommen, beeinträchtigt oder die Wahrnehmung von deren Aufgaben erschwert.* |
| **Norway** | Barnelova § 42 (1) | *Foreldra har gjensidig ansvar for at samværsretten vert oppfylt.* (English official: *The parents have mutual responsibility for implementing the right of access.*) |
| **Sweden** | Föräldrabalken kap 6 § 15 (3) | *Barnets föräldrar har ett gemensamt ansvar för att barnets behov av umgänge med en förälder som barnet inte bor tillsammans med så långt möjligt tillgodoses.* |
| **Iceland** | Barnalög art. 46 (implementing practice) | *Barn á rétt á að umgangast með reglubundnum hætti það foreldra sinna sem það býr ekki hjá.* (joint-responsibility implementing duty per *Sýslumaður* guidance) |

## Structural typology

The five frameworks share the same operative target — impairment of the child's relationship with the other parent — but cluster into two structural patterns:

### Type A — Standalone affirmative-duty clause (Austria, Norway, Sweden)

The cooperation duty is a **standalone provision** that operates independently of the contact-right framework. The duty applies whether or not there is an active contact dispute. In Austria, ABGB § 159 applies to *all* parental-responsibility rights and duties; in Norway, Barnelova § 42 frames it as a mutual-responsibility clause that runs alongside the child's contact right; in Sweden, Föräldrabalken kap 6 § 15 (3) frames it as a joint-responsibility clause for ensuring contact-need is met.

The doctrinal advantage of Type A: alienating conduct is actionable as a freestanding breach of duty, not merely as a contact-restriction trigger.

### Type B — Sub-clause within the contact-right provision (Germany)

The cooperation duty is **embedded within** the contact-right provision. BGB § 1684 (1) establishes the child's contact right and each parent's contact entitlement; § 1684 (2) imposes the *Wohlverhaltensklausel* as a sub-clause regulating *how* that right is exercised.

The doctrinal advantage of Type B: tighter analytical coupling between contact and conduct — breach of the duty is directly tied to contact-restriction analysis under § 1684 (3) and (4).

Iceland's framework is Type A in structure but with the cooperation duty operationalised through implementing practice rather than express statutory text — closer to the Norwegian model.

## Comparative analysis — alienation-conduct application

### Operative remedy ladder

Across all five jurisdictions, the cooperation-duty breach is operative without requiring proof of the *syndromal* construct of PAS. The remedy ladder is broadly similar:

1. **Counselling/mediation order** — DE *Erziehungsberatung* (§ 156 FamFG); AT *Erziehungsberatung* (§ 107 AußStrG); NO *familievernkontor mekling*; SE *samarbetssamtal*; IS *Sýslumaður* art. 33 a *sáttamiðlun*
2. **Contact-arrangement modification** — supervised contact, structured handovers, third-party intermediary
3. **Cooperation-pflegschaft / contact custodian** — DE Umgangspflegschaft (§ 1684 (3) BGB); AT analogous via § 187 (2) ABGB
4. **Custody reallocation** — moves residence/decision-making authority to the targeted parent
5. **In extreme sustained cases** — full *decadenza* / deprivation of parental responsibility (Italy art. 330 CC; DE § 1666 BGB; AT § 181 ABGB)

### Asymmetry between jurisdictions

- **Norway uniquely codifies re-litigation right.** Barnelova § 43 final paragraph: *If the parent who has custody, or with whom the child lives, prevents implementation of access, the other parent may request that the question of parental responsibility, or who the child shall live with, be reconsidered.* No equivalent express clause in DE/AT/SE/IS — those jurisdictions reach the same outcome through general welfare-assessment provisions.
- **Sweden uniquely operationalises the cooperation test in custody allocation.** Föräldrabalken kap 6 § 5 (2) makes the parents' *ability to put the child's needs first and take joint responsibility* the explicit custody-allocation factor.
