# EU Charter of Fundamental Rights (Charter of Nice / EU CFR 2000)

The EU Charter of Fundamental Rights (popularly the EU CFR / formally Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union) is a European Union supranational meta-framework EU bloc primary-law human-rights instrument adopted by the European Parliament + Council + Commission — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework operating EU bloc primary-law human-rights framework (EU Charter of Fundamental Rights adopted 7 December 2000 in Nice + entered force 1 December 2009 alongside Treaty of Lisbon framework — formal EU bloc primary-law human-rights framework with formal binding legal effect on EU member states when implementing EU law, distinct from ECHR framework which operates as Council of Europe regional framework + UN human-rights frameworks which operate as universal multilateral frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework operating EU bloc primary-law human-rights framework with formal primacy over EU member state domestic law when implementing EU law), as the central jurisdiction of the formal CFR Article 7 (Respect for Private and Family Life) + Article 24 (Rights of the Child) framework (CFR Article 7 framework on respect for private and family life + Article 24 framework on rights of the child — formal EU bloc primary-law family-rights + children's-rights dual framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework operating EU bloc primary-law family-rights + children's-rights dual framework with formal binding legal effect), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) Charter case-law framework (CJEU Charter case-law framework via formal CJEU jurisdiction over EU Charter framework including landmark cases C-540/03 Parliament v Council 2006 + C-491/10 PPU Aguirre Zarraga 2010 + C-498/14 PPU Bradbrooke 2015 + multiple subsequent cases — formal CJEU Charter case-law framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-developed modern supranational meta-framework CJEU case-law framework on EU bloc primary-law human-rights framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal post-Lisbon Treaty CFR entry-into-force framework (CFR adopted 7 December 2000 + entered force 1 December 2009 alongside Treaty of Lisbon framework — formal post-Lisbon Treaty CFR entry-into-force framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework that became binding via Lisbon Treaty framework). The CFR is binding upon all 27 EU member states when implementing EU law. The CFR is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 7 + Article 24 frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via EU Brussels IIa Regulation 2201/2003 + EU Council Regulation 2019/1111 framework. The Court of Justice of the European Union is the apex judicial body framework; national-level apex courts also operate as judicial bodies via formal preliminary reference framework.
