# Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region (宁夏回族自治区 / نٍ شِيَا خُوِذُو زِجِ‌ٿِيُوِ)

The Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region (宁夏回族自治区 Níngxià Huízú Zìzhìqū in Chinese / نٍ شِيَا خُوِذُو زِجِ‌ٿِيُوِ in Xiao'erjing Arabic-script Chinese) is a Northwestern Chinese civil-law Chinese autonomous-region constituent unit of the People's Republic of China — structurally distinctive globally as the only Chinese autonomous-region established on the basis of Hui Sino-Muslim ethnic-religious framework (~36% Hui Sino-Muslim population — distinct from rest-of-China's Han-cultural-political framework, with Hui as Han-Chinese-speaking Sunni Muslim ethnic-religious community descended from Silk Road Arab + Persian + Central Asian Muslim trader frameworks 7th-13th centuries), as the smallest Chinese autonomous-region by area (~66,400 km² — ~0.7% of Chinese land area, smallest of all 5 Chinese autonomous regions), as the central jurisdiction of the modern Hui Sino-Muslim cultural-religious framework (Tongxin Great Mosque + Najiahu Mosque + Yinchuan Sino-Muslim architectural framework — distinctive Hui-Muslim mosque architectural tradition combining Chinese imperial + Arabic-Islamic architectural elements, distinct from Uyghur Turkic-Central-Asian-Muslim mosque architectural tradition in Xinjiang), and as the only modern Chinese sub-national territory operating Xiao'erjing نٍ شِيَا (Arabic-script Chinese writing system historically used by Hui Sino-Muslim communities — distinct from rest-of-Chinese-speaking world). Ningxia historically operated under multiple frameworks including Western Xia 1038-1227 (Tangut Empire) + Mongol-Yuan 1227-1368 + Ming-Qing Ningxia Wei military framework, was established as Ningxia Province 1929-1954, merged into Gansu Province 1954-1958, and was formally established as Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region 25 October 1958. Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region comprises ~66,400 km² with a population of ~7.2 million (~2.5 million Hui + ~4.5 million Han + others). Family-law framework operates under the federal Chinese civil-law framework (Chinese Marriage Law 1980 + Chinese Civil Code 2020 Family Law portion Articles 1040-1118) applied uniformly across Chinese autonomous regions. The Ningxia High People's Court is the apex regional court; final appellate jurisdiction lies with the Supreme People's Court of China. Ningxia is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. China is a Hague Convention 1980 signatory but not a party — Ningxia is governed by the Chinese non-ratification status.
