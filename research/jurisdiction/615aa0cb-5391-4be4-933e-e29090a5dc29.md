# Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region (广西壮族自治区 / Gvangjsih Bouxcuengh Swcigih)

The Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region (广西壮族自治区 Guǎngxī Zhuàngzú Zìzhìqū in Chinese / Gvangjsih Bouxcuengh Swcigih in Romanised Zhuang) is a Southern Chinese civil-law Chinese autonomous-region constituent unit of the People's Republic of China — structurally distinctive globally as the only Chinese autonomous-region established on the basis of Tai-Kadai-language-family Zhuang Bouxcuengh ethnic-identity framework (Zhuang Bouxcuengh comprise ~18 million population — largest non-Han ethnic minority population in China, speaking Northern Zhuang + Southern Zhuang Tai-Kadai languages distinct from Sino-Tibetan Chinese-language framework), as the only modern Chinese sub-national territory operating Romanised Zhuang script Gvangjsih Bouxcuengh Swcigih in official government framework (Zhuang Romanised script developed 1957 and revised 1982 — modern Latin-alphabet writing system distinct from rest-of-Chinese-speaking world's Chinese-character framework), as the only Chinese autonomous-region with international land border with Vietnam (~1,020 km Guangxi-Vietnam border + Friendship Pass / Hữu Nghị Quan historic Sino-Vietnamese border crossing framework), and as the central jurisdiction of historical Bach Viet 百越 / Baak-Yueq pre-Sinitic Tai-Kadai cultural-linguistic framework (~3rd millennium BCE - 3rd century CE Bach Viet framework spanning modern Guangxi + Guangdong + Northern Vietnam — pre-Sinitic Tai-Kadai cultural-linguistic substrate continuous through modern Zhuang identity framework). Guangxi historically operated under multiple frameworks including Bach Viet pre-Sinitic framework + Qin-Han Lingnan Commandery 214 BCE - 3rd century CE + Tang-Song Guangnan Circuit + Ming-Qing Guangxi Province, was established as Guangxi Province 1912-1958, and was formally re-established as Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region 5 March 1958. Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region comprises ~237,600 km² with a population of ~50 million (~18 million Zhuang + ~30 million Han + others). Family-law framework operates under the federal Chinese civil-law framework (Chinese Marriage Law 1980 + Chinese Civil Code 2020 Family Law portion Articles 1040-1118) applied uniformly across Chinese autonomous regions. The Guangxi High People's Court is the apex regional court; final appellate jurisdiction lies with the Supreme People's Court of China. Guangxi is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. China is a Hague Convention 1980 signatory but not a party — Guangxi is governed by the Chinese non-ratification status.
