# UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC)

The UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC) is a global supranational meta-framework children's-rights binding-treaty instrument adopted by the UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on children's-rights framework with formal universal-coverage framework (UNCRC adopted 20 November 1989 by UN General Assembly Resolution 44/25 + effective 2 September 1990 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive children's-rights framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-human-rights frameworks which operate on broader general-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the most-widely-adopted UN human-rights treaty framework in human history), as the most-widely-adopted UN human-rights treaty framework in human history (UNCRC has 196 state parties as of 2026 — universal coverage except United States, which signed 16 February 1995 but did not ratify, structurally distinctive globally as the most-widely-adopted UN human-rights treaty framework in human history, ahead of CRPD 192 state parties + CEDAW 189 state parties + UNCAT 173 state parties), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 3 Best Interests of the Child framework (Convention Article 3(1) framework requiring 'best interests of the child shall be a primary consideration' in all actions concerning children — formal foundational best-interests-of-the-child framework underlying all subsequent modern international + regional + sub-national children's-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the most-fundamental modern children's-rights framework provision), and as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern UNCRC Committee General Comment frameworks globally (UNCRC Committee General Comment 12 on the Right of the Child to be Heard 2009 + General Comment 14 on the Right of the Child to Have His or Her Best Interests Taken as a Primary Consideration 2013 + General Comment 25 on Children's Rights in Relation to the Digital Environment 2021 + multiple subsequent General Comments — formal UNCRC Committee General Comment framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-children's-rights frameworks operating without formal General Comment framework). The UNCRC is binding upon 196 state parties as of 2026 — universal coverage except United States. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 3 Best Interests + Article 9 Separation from Parents + Article 12 Right to be Heard + Article 18 Common Responsibilities + Article 19 Protection from Harm + Article 27 Standard of Living frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework. The UNCRC Committee is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies. The UNCRC is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label.
