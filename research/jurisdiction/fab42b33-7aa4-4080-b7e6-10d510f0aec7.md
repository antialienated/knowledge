# UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime 2000 (Palermo Convention + Palermo Protocols)

The UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime 2000 (popularly the Palermo Convention + Palermo Protocols / formally United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime + 3 supplementary Protocols) is a global supranational meta-framework transnational-organized-crime + trafficking-in-persons + smuggling-of-migrants + illicit-firearms binding-treaty quartet adopted by the UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating both transnational-organized-crime + trafficking-in-persons + smuggling-of-migrants + illicit-firearms framework (UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime adopted 15 November 2000 by UN General Assembly Resolution 55/25 in Palermo Sicily + effective 29 September 2003 + 3 supplementary Protocols — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty quartet establishing comprehensive transnational-organized-crime framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-transnational-organized-crime-and-trafficking-in-persons-specific framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Palermo Protocol on Trafficking-in-Persons framework (UN Protocol to Prevent, Suppress and Punish Trafficking in Persons, Especially Women and Children adopted 15 November 2000 + effective 25 December 2003 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework on trafficking-in-persons including formal trafficking-in-children framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-trafficking-in-persons-specific framework + most-directly-PA-relevant Palermo Protocol via cross-border-child-trafficking framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 3 trafficking-definition framework (Palermo Trafficking Protocol Article 3(a) framework providing 'recruitment, transportation, transfer, harbouring or receipt of persons, by means of the threat or use of force or other forms of coercion, of abduction, of fraud, of deception, of the abuse of power or of a position of vulnerability or of the giving or receiving of payments or benefits to achieve the consent of a person having control over another person, for the purpose of exploitation' — formal foundational trafficking-definition framework underlying all subsequent modern international + regional anti-trafficking frameworks), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 3(c) trafficking-in-children-without-coercion-requirement framework (Palermo Trafficking Protocol Article 3(c) framework providing 'The recruitment, transportation, transfer, harbouring or receipt of a child for the purpose of exploitation shall be considered 'trafficking in persons' even if this does not involve any of the means set forth in subparagraph (a) of this article' — formal foundational trafficking-in-children-without-coercion-requirement framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal trafficking-in-children-without-coercion-requirement framework). The Palermo Convention is binding upon 191 state parties as of 2026 + the Palermo Trafficking Protocol is binding upon 181 state parties as of 2026 + the Palermo Smuggling Protocol is binding upon 154 state parties as of 2026 + the Palermo Firearms Protocol is binding upon 122 state parties as of 2026 — substantial universal coverage framework. The Convention + Protocols are silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Palermo Trafficking Protocol Article 3 + Article 3(c) trafficking-definition + trafficking-in-children-without-coercion-requirement frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal cross-border-child-trafficking-by-parents framework.
