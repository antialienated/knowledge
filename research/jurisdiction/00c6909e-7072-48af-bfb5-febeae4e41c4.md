# UN Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness 1961 + UN Convention relating to the Status of Stateless Persons 1954

The UN Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness 1961 + UN Convention relating to the Status of Stateless Persons 1954 (popularly the Statelessness Conventions / formally Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness adopted 30 August 1961 + Convention relating to the Status of Stateless Persons adopted 28 September 1954) is a global supranational meta-framework statelessness binding-treaty duo adopted by the UN General Assembly + UN Conference of Plenipotentiaries — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on statelessness framework (Convention 1961 + Convention 1954 framework — Convention 1954 adopted 28 September 1954 by UN Conference of Plenipotentiaries on the Status of Stateless Persons + effective 6 June 1960 + Convention 1961 adopted 30 August 1961 by UN Conference of Plenipotentiaries on the Elimination or Reduction of Future Statelessness + effective 13 December 1975 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty duo establishing comprehensive statelessness framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-human-rights frameworks which operate on broader general-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-statelessness-specific framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Convention 1961 Article 1 children's-nationality-at-birth framework (Convention 1961 Article 1 framework providing 'A Contracting State shall grant its nationality to a person born in its territory who would otherwise be stateless' — formal foundational children's-nationality-at-birth framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal children's-nationality-at-birth framework, with substantial relevance to PA framework via formal cross-border children-of-stateless-parents framework + children-of-different-citizenship-parents framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Convention 1954 Article 12 personal-status framework (Convention 1954 Article 12 framework providing 'The personal status of a stateless person shall be governed by the law of the country of his domicile' — formal foundational personal-status framework for stateless persons including formal marriage + family-law + children's-rights framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal personal-status framework for stateless persons), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal UNHCR Statelessness Mandate framework (UNHCR Statelessness Mandate established by UN General Assembly Resolutions 50/152 1995 + 61/137 2006 — formal UNHCR Statelessness Mandate framework operating identification + prevention + reduction + protection of stateless persons framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern UN agency framework with formal Statelessness Mandate). The Convention 1961 is binding upon 81 state parties as of 2026 + Convention 1954 is binding upon 99 state parties as of 2026 — substantial state party + non-state-party framework reflecting growing but limited adoption framework. The Conventions are silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Convention 1961 Article 1 children's-nationality-at-birth + Convention 1954 Article 12 personal-status frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal cross-border children-of-different-citizenship-parents framework. The UN High Commissioner for Refugees UNHCR is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies.
