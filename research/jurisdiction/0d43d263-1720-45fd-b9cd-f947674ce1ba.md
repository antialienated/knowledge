# UN Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW)

The UN Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW) is a global supranational meta-framework women's-rights binding-treaty instrument adopted by the UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on women's-rights framework (CEDAW adopted 18 December 1979 by UN General Assembly Resolution 34/180 + effective 3 September 1981 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive women's-rights framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-children's-rights frameworks which operate on broader best-interests + general-children's-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-women's-rights-specific framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal UN post-1979 women's-rights elaboration framework (CEDAW adopted as universal women's-rights binding-treaty framework — applicable to 189 state parties as of 2026, third-most-widely-adopted UN human-rights treaty framework after UNCRC 1989 196 state parties + Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities CRPD 2006 192 state parties, with substantial reservations framework reflecting wide adoption but limited harmonisation), as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern feminist-PA-critique frameworks globally (CEDAW General Recommendation 35 of 14 July 2017 on gender-based violence against women + CEDAW Committee individual communications framework documenting parental-alienation-syndrome-PAS as gender-based violence framework + 2018-2022 multiple CEDAW Committee Concluding Observations critiquing state-party use of PAS framework — UN-formal-feminist-critique-of-PAS framework, distinct from PA recognition frameworks), and as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern Special Rapporteur frameworks globally (UN Special Rapporteur on Violence Against Women Reem Alsalem 2022+ Report Custody, Violence Against Women and Violence Against Children A/HRC/53/36 13 June 2023 — formal UN Special Rapporteur critique of PAS framework + parental-alienation framework as gender-based violence framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-significant modern UN Special Rapporteur PA-critique framework). The CEDAW is binding upon 189 state parties as of 2026 — universal coverage except United States + Iran + Somalia + Sudan + Palau + Tonga + Holy See (excluded from CEDAW signatures + accessions framework). The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal women's-rights framework operates significant PA-critique framework via CEDAW General Recommendation 35 + Reem Alsalem Special Rapporteur Report frameworks. The CEDAW Committee is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies. The CEDAW is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label.
