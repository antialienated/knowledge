# Hague Convention 1980 on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction

The Hague Convention 1980 on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction (formally Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction) is a global supranational meta-framework cross-border-child-abduction-resolution binding-treaty instrument adopted by the Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH) — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on cross-border-child-abduction-resolution framework (Hague Convention 1980 adopted 25 October 1980 + effective 1 December 1983 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive cross-border-child-abduction-resolution framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-children's-rights frameworks which operate on broader best-interests + general-children's-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-cross-border-child-abduction-resolution-specific framework + most directly relevant Hague framework to parental-alienation framework via cross-border-PA-intersection), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 13(b) grave-risk exception framework that intersects with PA framework (Convention Article 13(b) framework providing for non-return exception where 'there is a grave risk that his or her return would expose the child to physical or psychological harm or otherwise place the child in an intolerable situation' — formal grave-risk exception framework operating substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal PA-as-Article-13(b)-grave-risk-claim framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most directly-PA-relevant Hague Convention framework provision), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Central Authority cooperation framework (Convention Article 6 Central Authority designation framework + Article 7 Central Authority duties framework + Article 14 application to Central Authority framework — formal Central-Authority-cooperation framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-children's-rights frameworks operating without formal Central-Authority-cooperation framework), and as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern cross-border-child-abduction-resolution case-law frameworks globally (multiple national-level apex court cases applying Article 13(b) grave-risk exception framework to PA framework + ECtHR Neulinger and Shuruk v. Switzerland 6 July 2010 + UN Special Rapporteur on Violence Against Women Reem Alsalem Report Custody, Violence Against Women and Violence Against Children A/HRC/53/36 13 June 2023 critique of Article 13(b)-applied-to-domestic-violence framework — formal multi-jurisdictional Article 13(b) case-law framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-developed modern cross-border-child-abduction-resolution case-law framework). The Hague Convention 1980 is binding upon 103 state parties as of 2026 — substantial state party + non-state-party framework reflecting widespread but not universal adoption framework. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 13(b) framework operates substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal PA-as-Article-13(b)-grave-risk-claim framework. The Permanent Bureau of the Hague Conference on Private International Law is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level Central Authorities + national-level apex courts operate as administrative + judicial bodies.
