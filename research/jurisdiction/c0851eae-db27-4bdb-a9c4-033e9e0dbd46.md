# Hague Convention 2005 on Choice of Court Agreements (Choice of Court Convention)

The Hague Convention 2005 on Choice of Court Agreements (popularly the Choice of Court Convention / formally Convention of 30 June 2005 on Choice of Court Agreements) is a global supranational meta-framework choice-of-court-agreements binding-treaty instrument adopted by the Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH) — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on cross-border-choice-of-court-agreements framework (Choice of Court Convention adopted 30 June 2005 in The Hague + effective 1 October 2015 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive choice-of-court-agreements framework via formal three-pillar exclusive-choice-of-court framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-cross-border-jurisdiction frameworks which operate as bilateral or regional frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-cross-border-choice-of-court-agreements-specific framework + most directly predecessor framework to Hague Judgments Convention 2019), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 2(2) family-law exclusion framework (Choice of Court Convention Article 2(2) framework providing exclusion of consumer + employment + family-law + insolvency-related matters from Convention applicability — formal family-law exclusion framework operating as complementary framework with Hague Convention 1980 + 1993 + 1996 + 2007 family-law-specific frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework with formal family-law exclusion framework predating Hague Judgments Convention 2019 Article 2 family-law exclusion framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal three-pillar exclusive-choice-of-court framework (Choice of Court Convention Article 5 framework on jurisdiction of chosen-court + Article 6 framework on jurisdiction obligations of courts other than chosen-court + Article 8 framework on recognition + enforcement of judgments of chosen-court — formal foundational three-pillar exclusive-choice-of-court framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal three-pillar exclusive-choice-of-court framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal EU bloc + UK + US + Singapore + Mexico + Israel + Russia + China + Montenegro state-party framework (Choice of Court Convention applicable to 6 state parties + 1 EU bloc state party as of 2026 — substantial state-party-coverage growth potential including formal post-Brexit UK independent accession framework in 2020). The Choice of Court Convention is binding upon 6 state parties as of 2026 + 1 EU bloc state party framework — early-adoption framework with substantial growth potential. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 2(2) family-law exclusion framework explicitly excludes family-law matters from Convention applicability — operating as complementary framework with Hague Convention 1980 + 1993 + 1996 + 2007 family-law-specific frameworks. The Permanent Bureau of the Hague Conference on Private International Law is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies.
