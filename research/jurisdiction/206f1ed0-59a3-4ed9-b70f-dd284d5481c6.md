# Andaman and Nicobar Islands (अंडमान और निकोबार द्वीप समूह)

The Andaman and Nicobar Islands (अंडमान और निकोबार द्वीप समूह in Hindi) are a Bay of Bengal mixed common-law/indigenous-customary-law Indian Union Territory comprising the Andaman Islands group (300+ islands) and Nicobar Islands group (22 islands) — structurally distinctive globally as the only Indian jurisdiction with an uncontacted indigenous tribe (the Sentinelese of North Sentinel Island, who have remained isolated for ~60,000+ years and whose contact is explicitly prohibited under the Aboriginal Tribes (Andaman and Nicobar Islands) Regulation 1956 and 1991+ Eyes-and-Ears policy — the only modern formal state-level prohibition on contact with an uncontacted indigenous tribe), as the central jurisdiction of the most extensive Indian indigenous-tribe-protection framework operating five Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs): Great Andamanese, Onge, Jarawa, Sentinelese (all Negrito-origin), and Shompen (Mongoloid Nicobarese), and as the only Indian Union Territory with comprehensive Inner Line Permit-equivalent restricted-area framework via Restricted Area Permit (RAP) under the Foreigners (Protected Areas) Order 1958. The Andamans were Cellular Jail penal colony framework (Kālā Pānī, 'Black Water') under British colonial framework 1858-1942, were occupied by Imperial Japan 1942-1945, were proclaimed as the temporary Provisional Government of Free India by Subhas Chandra Bose's Azad Hind on 30 December 1943 (the only Indian territory under formal Azad Hind administration during WWII), and integrated into India following 1947. Andaman and Nicobar Islands comprise ~8,249 km² total area across ~500+ islands with a population of ~380,000 (~95% non-indigenous, ~5% indigenous tribal). Family-law framework operates under the federal Indian common-law framework (Hindu Marriage Act 1955, Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act 1937, Guardians and Wards Act 1890) applied via Union Territory administrative framework with Aboriginal Tribes Regulation 1956 customary-law protection for the five PVTGs. The Calcutta High Court (Port Blair Circuit Bench) is the apex domestic appellate court; final appellate jurisdiction lies with the Supreme Court of India. Andaman and Nicobar Islands are silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. India is a Hague Convention 1980 signatory but not a party — Andaman and Nicobar Islands are governed by the Indian non-ratification status. Parental-alienation recognition status: [object Object].
