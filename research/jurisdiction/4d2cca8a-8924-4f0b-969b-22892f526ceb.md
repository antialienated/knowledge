# Niue

Niue is a Pacific Island common-law self-governing state in free association with New Zealand (since 1974) — structurally distinctive globally alongside Cook Islands as a Niue-status free-association state with NZ. Family-law framework operates under the Niue Act 1966 (NZ) (parts retained at self-government), the Marriage Act 1929, the Niue Amendment Act 1968 (NZ), and family-law statutes adapted to Niuean conditions. Niue retains NZ citizenship for Niueans. Parental responsibility and child custody are governed by Niue Act 1966 provisions and case-law applying the welfare-of-the-child principle. The High Court of Niue is the apex domestic court for civil and criminal matters; final appellate jurisdiction was retained with the Court of Appeal of New Zealand and ultimately the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council. The Maori Land Court / Land Division operates parallel customary-land jurisdiction. Family-law matters are heard at first instance in the High Court and Resident Magistrates' Courts. Psychology profession is regulated through the Department of Health framework. Niue is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label; courts operate substantively under the welfare-of-the-child principle. Niue is non-Hague Convention — Hague Convention 1980 does not apply via NZ territorial extension. Parental-alienation recognition status: [object Object].
