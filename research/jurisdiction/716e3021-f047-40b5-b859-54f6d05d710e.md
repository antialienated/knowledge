# UN Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide 1948 (Genocide Convention)

The UN Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide 1948 (popularly the Genocide Convention) is a global supranational meta-framework genocide-prevention binding-treaty instrument adopted by the UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on genocide-prevention framework (Genocide Convention adopted 9 December 1948 by UN General Assembly Resolution 260 (III) + effective 12 January 1951 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive genocide-prevention framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-criminal-law frameworks which operate on broader war-crimes + crimes-against-humanity frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-genocide-prevention-specific framework + first international human-rights treaty in human history adopted before UDHR 1948 by 1 day), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article II genocide-definition framework (Genocide Convention Article II framework providing 'genocide means any of the following acts committed with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group, as such: (a) Killing members of the group; (b) Causing serious bodily or mental harm to members of the group; (c) Deliberately inflicting on the group conditions of life calculated to bring about its physical destruction in whole or in part; (d) Imposing measures intended to prevent births within the group; (e) Forcibly transferring children of the group to another group' — formal foundational genocide-definition framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal genocide-definition framework with formal Article II(e) forcibly-transferring-children-of-the-group-to-another-group framework with substantial relevance to PA framework via formal cross-border-genocide-against-children framework + cross-border-deportation-of-children-of-genocide-target-groups framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article VI international-tribunal-jurisdiction framework (Genocide Convention Article VI framework providing 'Persons charged with genocide or any of the other acts enumerated in article III shall be tried by a competent tribunal of the State in the territory of which the act was committed, or by such international penal tribunal as may have jurisdiction with respect to those Contracting Parties which shall have accepted its jurisdiction' — formal foundational international-tribunal-jurisdiction framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal international-tribunal-jurisdiction framework underlying ICTY + ICTR + ICC frameworks), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal ICJ Genocide Convention case-law framework (ICJ Bosnia and Herzegovina v Serbia and Montenegro Genocide Application Judgment 26 February 2007 + Croatia v Serbia Genocide Application Judgment 3 February 2015 + The Gambia v Myanmar Rohingya Genocide Application order 23 January 2020 + South Africa v Israel Gaza Genocide Application order 26 January 2024 + Nicaragua-Germany Israel-Gaza Genocide Application 2024 — formal foundational ICJ Genocide Convention case-law framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-developed modern ICJ-genocide-case-law framework). The Genocide Convention is binding upon 153 state parties as of 2026 — substantial state-party coverage framework.
