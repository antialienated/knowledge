# UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD)

The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) is a global supranational meta-framework disability-rights binding-treaty instrument adopted by the UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on disability-rights framework (CRPD adopted 13 December 2006 by UN General Assembly Resolution 61/106 + effective 3 May 2008 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive disability-rights framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-children's-rights frameworks which operate on broader best-interests + general-children's-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-disability-rights-specific framework + 21st-century-adopted UN human rights treaty framework), as the second-most-widely-adopted UN human-rights treaty framework after UN Convention on the Rights of the Child UNCRC 1989 (CRPD has 192 state parties as of 2026 — second-most-widely-adopted UN human-rights treaty after UNCRC 196 state parties, ahead of CEDAW 189 state parties + UNCAT 173 state parties + ICCPR 174 state parties + ICESCR 173 state parties, structurally distinctive globally as the second-most-widely-adopted UN human-rights treaty), as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern disability-rights elaboration frameworks globally (Convention Article 23 framework on respect for home and the family + Article 24 framework on education + Article 25 framework on health + Article 30 framework on participation in cultural life — formal comprehensive disability-rights framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-disability frameworks operating on broader equal-treatment or accessibility frameworks without formal Article 23 framework on respect for home and the family), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal CRPD Optional Protocol individual communications framework (CRPD Optional Protocol adopted 13 December 2006 + effective 3 May 2008 — formal individual communications framework via CRPD Committee, applicable to 105 state parties as of 2026, with CRPD Committee individual communications framework documenting parental-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities framework + child-protection-framework-of-children-with-disabilities framework). The CRPD is binding upon 192 state parties as of 2026 — universal coverage except United States + Russia + Hungary + Marshall Islands. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 23 framework on respect for home and the family operates significant intersection with parental-rights framework. The CRPD Committee is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies. The CRPD is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label.
