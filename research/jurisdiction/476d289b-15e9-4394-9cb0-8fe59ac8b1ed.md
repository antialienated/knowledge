# EU Brussels IIa Regulation (Brussels IIb / EU Council Regulation 2201/2003 + Recast 2019/1111)

The EU Brussels IIa Regulation (now formally Brussels IIb post-2022 / formally EU Council Regulation 2201/2003 of 27 November 2003 + Recast EU Council Regulation 2019/1111 of 25 June 2019) is a European Union supranational meta-framework EU bloc family-law + parental-responsibility + cross-border-child-abduction-resolution binding instrument adopted by the European Council — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework operating EU bloc binding family-law + parental-responsibility + cross-border-child-abduction-resolution framework (Brussels IIa Regulation 2201/2003 adopted 27 November 2003 + effective 1 March 2005 + Brussels IIb Recast Regulation 2019/1111 adopted 25 June 2019 + effective 1 August 2022 — formal EU bloc binding family-law framework establishing jurisdiction + recognition + enforcement framework for matrimonial matters + parental-responsibility + cross-border-child-abduction-resolution within EU bloc, distinct from Hague Convention 1980 universal multilateral framework which operates outside EU bloc framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework operating EU bloc binding family-law framework with direct effect within EU bloc), as the central jurisdiction of the formal EU bloc cross-border-child-abduction-resolution framework operating in concurrent applicability framework with Hague Convention 1980 (Brussels IIb Recast Regulation Articles 22-29 framework on cross-border-child-abduction-resolution + Article 23 recognition framework + Article 24 enforcement framework + Article 27 grounds for non-recognition framework — formal EU bloc cross-border-child-abduction-resolution framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern EU bloc framework operating in concurrent applicability framework with Hague Convention 1980 within EU bloc), as the central jurisdiction of the formal CJEU Brussels IIa + IIb case-law framework (CJEU Brussels IIa case-law framework via landmark cases C-491/10 PPU Aguirre Zarraga 2010 + C-498/14 PPU Bradbrooke 2015 + C-256/09 Purrucker 2010 + C-211/10 PPU Povse 2010 + multiple subsequent CJEU cases — formal CJEU Brussels IIa + IIb case-law framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-developed modern supranational meta-framework CJEU case-law framework on EU bloc binding family-law + parental-responsibility + cross-border-child-abduction-resolution framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal 2019/2022 Brussels IIb Recast framework (Recast EU Council Regulation 2019/1111 adopted 25 June 2019 + effective 1 August 2022 — formal Recast Brussels IIb framework substantially revising original Brussels IIa Regulation 2201/2003 framework via formal recasted-jurisdiction framework + formal recasted-recognition framework + formal recasted-enforcement framework + formal recasted-cross-border-child-abduction-resolution framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework with formal recast framework on EU bloc binding family-law framework). The Brussels IIa/IIb Regulation is binding upon all 26 EU member states excluding Denmark (Denmark opted out via formal Schengen Protocol framework). The Regulation is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal framework operates substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal cross-border-child-abduction-resolution + custody-determination + EU bloc Hague Convention 1980 Article 13(b) interpretation framework. The Court of Justice of the European Union is the apex judicial body framework via formal preliminary reference framework + national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies.
