# UN Universal Periodic Review (UPR / UN Human Rights Council Universal Periodic Review)

The UN Universal Periodic Review (UPR) is a global supranational meta-framework UN Human Rights Council peer-review mechanism — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral peer-review-of-all-states human-rights framework (UPR established 15 March 2006 by UN General Assembly Resolution 60/251 establishing UN Human Rights Council UNHRC + first cycle commenced 7 April 2008 — formal UN peer-review-of-all-states human-rights framework, distinct from UN treaty-body frameworks which operate state-party-only frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral peer-review-of-all-states human-rights framework with formal universal coverage), as the central jurisdiction of the formal UN Human Rights Council Universal Periodic Review Mechanism (UPR Cycle 1 2008-2011 + Cycle 2 2012-2016 + Cycle 3 2017-2021 + Cycle 4 2022-2026 + Cycle 5 2027-2031 — formal four-and-a-half-year cycle peer-review framework with all 193 UN member states reviewed in each cycle, structurally distinctive globally as the most-systematic modern human-rights peer-review framework), as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern UN human-rights mechanism frameworks operating both formal universal-coverage framework + formal state-state peer-review framework + formal civil-society + NHRI stakeholder framework (UPR operates formal UN Member State + National Human Rights Institution NHRI + Civil Society + UN treaty-body + UN Special Procedures stakeholder framework — formal multi-stakeholder framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-UN-human-rights frameworks operating without formal multi-stakeholder framework), and as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern UN UPR PA-relevant framework reviews globally (multiple UPR Country Reports addressing PA-relevant framework via formal recommendations on children's rights + family violence + women's rights + custody-determination + Article 13(b) Hague Convention 1980 application frameworks — formal multi-jurisdictional UPR PA-relevant framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-UN-human-rights frameworks operating without formal multi-jurisdictional PA-relevant framework). The UPR is binding upon all 193 UN member states + observer states as formal universal participation framework. The UPR is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal recommendations and concluding observations document substantial PA-relevant framework via children's rights + family violence + women's rights + custody-determination + Hague Convention 1980 Article 13(b) frameworks. The UN Human Rights Council is the formal monitoring framework body; the Office of the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights OHCHR is the formal Secretariat framework body.
