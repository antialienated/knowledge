# UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS 1982)

The UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) is a global supranational meta-framework law-of-the-sea binding-treaty instrument adopted by the UN Third Conference on the Law of the Sea — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on law-of-the-sea framework (UNCLOS adopted 10 December 1982 in Montego Bay Jamaica + effective 16 November 1994 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive law-of-the-sea framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-treaty frameworks which operate as substantive treaties on terrestrial-jurisdiction matters, structurally distinctive globally as the 'constitution for the oceans' framework + first major UN comprehensive law-of-the-sea treaty framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Part II Territorial Sea + Part V Exclusive Economic Zone EEZ frameworks (UNCLOS Part II Articles 2-33 + Part V Articles 55-75 framework providing formal 12-nautical-mile territorial sea + 200-nautical-mile EEZ framework — formal foundational maritime-zone framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal comprehensive maritime-zone framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Part XV Dispute Settlement Framework (UNCLOS Part XV Articles 279-299 framework providing formal compulsory dispute-settlement framework via International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea ITLOS + ICJ + Annex VII arbitration + Annex VIII special arbitration — formal foundational law-of-the-sea dispute-settlement framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal compulsory dispute-settlement framework with four-tier framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal Part XI International Seabed Area + International Seabed Authority ISA framework (UNCLOS Part XI Articles 133-191 framework + 1994 Implementation Agreement providing formal common-heritage-of-mankind framework for international seabed area beyond national jurisdiction — formal foundational seabed framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal common-heritage-of-mankind framework). The UNCLOS is binding upon 169 state parties as of 2026 — substantial universal coverage framework (notable non-party: United States). The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Part XV dispute-settlement framework operates substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal cross-border maritime-jurisdiction-disputes-affecting-cross-border-families framework. The International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea ITLOS + International Seabed Authority ISA + Commission on the Limits of the Continental Shelf CLCS are the formal UNCLOS bodies; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies. The UNCLOS is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Parental-alienation recognition status: [object Object].
