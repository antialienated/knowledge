# UN Convention against Torture (UNCAT 1984)

The UN Convention against Torture and Other Cruel, Inhuman or Degrading Treatment or Punishment (UNCAT) is a global supranational meta-framework torture + ill-treatment binding-treaty instrument adopted by the UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on torture + cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment framework (UNCAT adopted 10 December 1984 by UN General Assembly Resolution 39/46 + effective 26 June 1987 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive torture + ill-treatment framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-human-rights frameworks which operate on broader general-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-torture-and-ill-treatment-specific framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 2 absolute-prohibition-of-torture framework (UNCAT Article 2(2) framework providing 'No exceptional circumstances whatsoever, whether a state of war or a threat of war, internal political instability or any other public emergency, may be invoked as a justification of torture' — formal absolute-prohibition-of-torture framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal absolute-prohibition-of-torture framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 14 right-to-redress + reparation framework (UNCAT Article 14 framework providing right of victim of an act of torture to obtain redress + reparation including fair and adequate compensation + as full rehabilitation as possible — formal right-to-redress + reparation framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal right-to-redress + reparation framework for torture victims, with substantial relevance to PA framework via formal children's-rights-victims-of-PA + family-violence redress framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal UNCAT Optional Protocol OPCAT 2002 individual visit framework (UNCAT Optional Protocol on the Prevention of Torture OPCAT adopted 18 December 2002 + effective 22 June 2006 — formal Subcommittee on Prevention of Torture SPT + National Preventive Mechanisms NPM individual visit framework, applicable to 95 state parties as of 2026, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal individual visit framework for torture prevention). The UNCAT is binding upon 173 state parties as of 2026 — fifth-most-widely-adopted UN human-rights treaty framework after UNCRC + CRPD + CEDAW + ICCPR. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 1 torture-definition + Article 2 absolute-prohibition + Article 14 right-to-redress frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal PA-as-child-psychological-abuse intersection framework + CAT Committee individual communications framework. The CAT Committee is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies. The UNCAT is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label.
