# Saint Lucia

Saint Lucia is a Caribbean mixed-legal-system constitutional monarchy combining French civil-law substantive heritage (via 17th-19th century French colonial inheritance — Civil Code of Saint Lucia 1879 drawing on Quebec/Lower Canadian codification) with English common-law procedural inheritance (post-1814 British administration). This is structurally distinctive within the corpus — Saint Lucia and Quebec share the French-civil-substantive + English-common-law-procedural hybrid pattern. Family-law framework operates under the Civil Code of Saint Lucia 1879 (CAP 4.01), the Divorce Act, the Children and Young Persons Act, and the Maintenance Act. Parental rights and child custody are governed by Civil Code arts. 218-247. The Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court (Court of Appeal sitting for Saint Lucia) is the apex domestic appellate court; final appellate jurisdiction was retained with the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council. Family-law matters are heard at first instance in the High Court (Family Division) and Magistrates' Courts. Psychology profession is regulated through the Ministry of Health framework. Saint Lucia is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label; courts operate substantively under the welfare-of-the-child principle. Saint Lucia is non-Hague Convention.
