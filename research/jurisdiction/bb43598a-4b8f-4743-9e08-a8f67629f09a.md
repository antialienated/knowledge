# Istanbul Convention (Council of Europe Convention on Preventing and Combating Violence Against Women and Domestic Violence 2011 / CETS 210)

The Istanbul Convention (Council of Europe Convention on Preventing and Combating Violence Against Women and Domestic Violence CETS 210) is a European supranational meta-framework gender-based-violence + domestic-violence binding-treaty instrument adopted by the Council of Europe — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern regional binding-treaty framework operating comprehensive gender-based-violence + domestic-violence framework with formal monitoring-body framework (Istanbul Convention adopted 11 May 2011 + effective 1 August 2014 — formal European regional binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive gender-based-violence + domestic-violence framework, distinct from CEDAW universal framework which operates broader women's-rights framework without formal monitoring-body framework + Inter-American Belém do Pará Convention 1994 which operates broader gender-based-violence framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern regional binding-treaty framework operating both formal binding-treaty framework + dedicated monitoring-body framework on gender-based-violence + domestic-violence), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Council of Europe post-CEDAW comprehensive-gender-based-violence framework elaboration (Istanbul Convention adopted as European regional supplement to CEDAW framework — applicable to 38 of 46 Council of Europe member states that have ratified the Convention as of 2026, with substantial state party + non-state-party European framework reflecting limited but-not-universal European-adoption framework), as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern Council of Europe Article 31 framework on custody-orders-after-domestic-violence framework (Convention Article 31 framework providing for state-party-obligation to take into account, in determining custody + visitation rights, formal incidents of violence against parent in custody-determination framework — formal direct intersection with parental-alienation framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-women's-rights frameworks which operate without formal custody-determination framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only formal regional binding-treaty Article 31 framework intersecting with parental-alienation framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the GREVIO monitoring framework (Group of Experts on Action against Violence against Women and Domestic Violence GREVIO established under Charter Article 66 — formal dedicated monitoring-body framework with formal periodic-evaluation + Country Report framework + Urgent Inquiry framework, distinct from CEDAW Committee framework operating without formal Article 31-equivalent custody-determination framework). The Istanbul Convention is binding upon 38 of 46 Council of Europe member states that have ratified the Convention as of 2026 (Turkey withdrew 1 July 2021 — first formal withdrawal from CoE human-rights treaty framework in Council of Europe history). The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 31 framework operates significant intersection with parental-alienation framework. The GREVIO is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies. The Istanbul Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label.
