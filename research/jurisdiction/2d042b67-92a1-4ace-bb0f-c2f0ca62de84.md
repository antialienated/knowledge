# Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR 1948)

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) is a global supranational meta-framework foundational human-rights soft-law instrument adopted by the UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as the foundational modern international human-rights instrument framework underlying all subsequent modern international + regional human-rights frameworks (UDHR adopted 10 December 1948 by UN General Assembly Resolution 217 A (III) in Palais de Chaillot, Paris — formal foundational human-rights soft-law instrument framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-human-rights frameworks which operate as binding-treaty frameworks subsequent to + influenced by UDHR framework, structurally distinctive globally as the foundational modern international human-rights instrument framework underlying all subsequent modern international + regional human-rights binding-treaty frameworks), as the most-translated document in human history (UDHR has been translated into 530+ languages as of 2026 — formal Guinness World Record as most-translated document in human history, structurally distinctive globally as the most-translated document in human history), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 16 family-rights framework (UDHR Article 16 framework on rights to marry and found a family + family-rights framework — formal foundational family-rights framework underlying all subsequent modern international + regional family-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the most-fundamental modern family-rights framework provision), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal post-WWII Eleanor Roosevelt-chaired UN Commission on Human Rights elaboration framework (UDHR drafted 1946-1948 by UN Commission on Human Rights chaired by Eleanor Roosevelt — formal UN drafting process framework, structurally distinctive globally as the formal foundational human-rights drafting process framework underlying all subsequent modern international human-rights frameworks). The UDHR is binding upon all UN member states as customary international law framework, with substantial debate on extent of binding-customary-international-law framework. The Declaration is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 16 family-rights framework operates substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework. The UDHR has no formal monitoring body framework — implementation framework operates via subsequent UN treaty bodies + Special Procedures + International Court of Justice frameworks.
