# Okinawa Prefecture (沖縄県 / 琉球 Ryukyu)

Okinawa Prefecture (沖縄県 Okinawa-ken in Japanese, also encompassing the Ryukyu 琉球 cultural identity) is a Southwestern Japanese civil-law Japanese prefecture comprising the Ryukyu Archipelago — structurally distinctive globally as the only Japanese prefecture with a continuous independent-kingdom framework predating Japanese incorporation (Ryukyu Kingdom 1429-1879 under the Sho Dynasty — independent maritime trading kingdom with tributary relationships with both Ming/Qing China and Tokugawa Japan via Satsuma Domain 1609-1879, distinct from rest-of-Japan's pre-Meiji feudal-domain framework), as the only Japanese prefecture subject to extended post-WWII US military administration (US Civil Administration of the Ryukyu Islands 1945-1972 — 27-year US administration following Battle of Okinawa April-June 1945 with ~150,000 civilian deaths, reverted to Japan 15 May 1972 under the Okinawa Reversion Agreement 1971), as the central jurisdiction for the US-Japan Status of Forces Agreement (SOFA) framework concentration (~70% of all US military base footprint in Japan concentrated in Okinawa Prefecture despite Okinawa comprising only ~0.6% of Japanese land area — Kadena Air Base + Marine Corps Air Station Futenma + Camp Schwab + Henoko relocation framework), and as a distinctive jurisdiction within Japan for Ryukyuan-cultural-identity-vs-Japanese-cultural-assimilation tension (Ryukyuan languages 沖縄口 Uchinaaguchi recognised by UNESCO as endangered languages 2009 — distinct from Japanese language but historically suppressed under Meiji-era assimilation policies 1879-1945). Okinawa was annexed by Meiji Japan via the Ryukyu Disposition framework 1872-1879 (abolishing the Ryukyu Kingdom and establishing Okinawa Prefecture 27 March 1879), suffered the Battle of Okinawa April-June 1945 (deadliest WWII Pacific battle on Japanese soil), was administered by the US Civil Administration of the Ryukyu Islands 1950-1972, and reverted to Japan on 15 May 1972. Okinawa Prefecture comprises ~2,281 km² (160+ islands across Ryukyu Archipelago) with a population of ~1.47 million. Family-law framework operates under the federal Japanese civil-law framework (Japanese Civil Code 1898 Family Law portion + Family Law Reform Act 2024 reintroducing joint parental authority post-divorce effective 2026) applied uniformly across Japanese prefectures. Parental authority (親権 shinken) and child custody operate under Japanese Civil Code framework — historically sole-custody framework until 2024 reform. The Naha District Court is the primary trial court; the Fukuoka High Court Naha Branch is the apex appellate court; final appellate jurisdiction lies with the Supreme Court of Japan. Okinawa is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Japan is a Hague Convention 1980 signatory and party (acceded 24 January 2014 effective 1 April 2014).
