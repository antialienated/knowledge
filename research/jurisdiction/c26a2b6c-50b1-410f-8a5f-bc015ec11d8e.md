# Hague Convention 2019 on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Judgments (Hague Judgments Convention)

The Hague Convention 2019 on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Judgments (popularly the Hague Judgments Convention / formally Convention of 2 July 2019 on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Judgments in Civil or Commercial Matters) is a global supranational meta-framework foreign-judgments-recognition + enforcement binding-treaty instrument adopted by the Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH) — structurally distinctive globally as the most-recent modern global binding-treaty framework operating specifically on cross-border foreign-judgments-recognition + enforcement framework (Hague Judgments Convention adopted 2 July 2019 in The Hague + effective 1 September 2023 — formal global binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive recognition + enforcement framework for foreign civil + commercial judgments, distinct from Hague Convention 1980 + 1993 + 1996 + 2007 children's-frameworks which operate on family-law-specific frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the most-recent modern global binding-treaty framework operating on formal-foreign-judgments-recognition-and-enforcement-civil-commercial-specific framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal post-Hague-2005 Choice of Court Convention judgments-recognition + enforcement elaboration framework (Hague Judgments Convention adopted as global comprehensive supplement to Hague Convention 2005 on Choice of Court Agreements framework + Hague Convention 2007 + Hague Convention 1996 frameworks — applicable to 6 state parties as of 2026 including European Union bloc + Ukraine + Uruguay + Israel + Cabo Verde + Costa Rica, structurally distinctive globally as the newest modern global binding-treaty framework with substantial state-party-coverage growth potential), as the only modern global binding-treaty framework operating formal exclusion of family-law + maintenance + insolvency frameworks (Convention Article 2 framework providing exclusion of family-law-related matters + maintenance obligations + insolvency-related matters from Convention applicability — formal family-law exclusion framework, distinct from Hague Convention 2007 + 1996 + 1993 + 1980 family-law-specific frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern global binding-treaty framework with formal family-law exclusion framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal EU bloc 2022 accession framework (European Union bloc accession to Hague Judgments Convention 29 August 2022 + effective 1 September 2023 alongside Convention entry into force — formal EU bloc-level accession framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern global binding-treaty framework with formal EU bloc-level accession framework). The Hague Judgments Convention is binding upon 6 state parties as of 2026 + 1 EU bloc state party framework — early-adoption framework with substantial growth potential. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 2 family-law exclusion framework explicitly excludes family-law matters from Convention applicability — operating as complementary framework with Hague Convention 1980 + 1993 + 1996 + 2007 frameworks. The Permanent Bureau of the Hague Conference on Private International Law is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies. The Hague Judgments Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Parental-alienation recognition status: [object Object].
