# Hague Convention 1993 on Intercountry Adoption

The Hague Convention 1993 on Intercountry Adoption (formally Convention on Protection of Children and Co-operation in Respect of Intercountry Adoption) is a global supranational meta-framework intercountry-adoption-cooperation instrument adopted by the Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH) — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern global binding-treaty framework operating specifically on intercountry-adoption framework (Hague Convention 1993 adopted 29 May 1993 + effective 1 May 1995 — formal global binding-treaty framework establishing cooperation framework for intercountry adoptions, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-children's-rights frameworks which operate on broader best-interests + general-children's-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern global binding-treaty framework operating on formal-intercountry-adoption-specific framework), as the only modern global binding-treaty framework operating formal Central-Authority-cooperation framework for intercountry adoption (Convention Article 6 Central Authority designation framework + Article 14 application to Central Authority framework + Article 17 acceptance framework + Article 23 recognition framework — formal Central-Authority-cooperation framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-children's-rights frameworks operating without formal Central-Authority-cooperation framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal HCCH post-1980 intercountry-adoption elaboration framework (Hague Convention 1993 adopted as global comprehensive supplement to Hague Convention 1980 + UNCRC 1989 frameworks — applicable to 105 state parties as of 2026, with substantial state parties from receiving + sending state parties, structurally distinctive globally as the most-widely-adopted Hague Convention framework on children-specific frameworks alongside Hague Convention 1980's 103 state parties), and as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern global intercountry-adoption frameworks globally (Convention Article 1 children's-rights-prioritisation framework + Article 4 child-eligibility framework + Article 5 prospective-adoptive-parents-eligibility framework + Article 16 child-suitability-evaluation framework + Article 17 acceptance-by-receiving-state framework + Article 21 trafficking-prevention framework — formal multi-stage global intercountry-adoption framework). The Hague Convention 1993 is binding upon 105 state parties as of 2026 including substantial receiving state parties (US + EU bloc + Australia + Canada + New Zealand + others) + sending state parties (China + India + Vietnam + Ethiopia + Russia + others). The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal intercountry-adoption framework is highly relevant. The Permanent Bureau of the Hague Conference on Private International Law is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level Central Authorities + national-level apex courts operate as administrative + judicial bodies. The Hague Convention 1993 is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Parental-alienation recognition status: [object Object].
