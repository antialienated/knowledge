# ILO Constitution 1919 (International Labour Organization)

The International Labour Organization (ILO) Constitution is a global supranational meta-framework labour-rights tripartite-structure binding-treaty instrument established by Part XIII of the Treaty of Versailles — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on labour-rights framework via formal tripartite-structure framework (ILO Constitution adopted 28 June 1919 as Part XIII of Treaty of Versailles + effective 10 January 1920 + 1944 Declaration of Philadelphia incorporated as Annex 1946 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive labour-rights framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-treaty frameworks which operate as binary state-state frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the oldest UN specialized agency + only UN specialized agency operating formal tripartite-structure framework with governments + employers + workers framework + first UN specialized agency framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal ILO Tripartite Structure framework (ILO Constitution Article 3 framework providing formal tripartite-structure with governments + employers + workers framework where each ILO member-state delegation consists of two government delegates + one employer delegate + one worker delegate — formal foundational tripartite-structure framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern UN specialized agency operating formal tripartite-structure framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal 1998 ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work framework (ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work adopted 18 June 1998 + amended 10 June 2022 to include occupational safety + health framework providing five fundamental principles: freedom of association + effective recognition of right to collective bargaining + elimination of forced labour + abolition of child labour + elimination of discrimination + occupational safety + health — formal foundational fundamental-labour-rights framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal fundamental-labour-rights framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal ILO 191 Conventions + 208 Recommendations framework (ILO 191 Conventions including 8 fundamental ILO Conventions + 4 governance ILO Conventions + 178 technical ILO Conventions + 208 Recommendations — formal foundational labour-standards framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating formal comprehensive labour-standards framework). The ILO Constitution is binding upon 187 ILO member states as of 2026 — substantial universal coverage framework. The Constitution is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal ILO Convention 156 Workers with Family Responsibilities 1981 + ILO Convention 183 Maternity Protection 2000 + ILO Convention 189 Domestic Workers 2011 frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal work-family-reconciliation framework. The ILO International Labour Conference ILC + ILO Governing Body + ILO International Labour Office Director-General + ILO Committee of Experts on the Application of Conventions and Recommendations CEACR + ILO Committee on Freedom of Association CFA are the formal ILO bodies; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies.
