# Inter-American Convention on Human Rights (Pact of San Jose / Pacto de San José de Costa Rica 1969)

The Inter-American Convention on Human Rights (popularly the Pact of San Jose / Pacto de San José de Costa Rica) is a Latin American supranational meta-framework human-rights binding-treaty instrument adopted by the Organization of American States (OAS) — structurally distinctive globally as one of three modern regional binding-treaty frameworks operating both formal human-rights binding-treaty framework + dedicated judicial-body framework (American Convention on Human Rights adopted 22 November 1969 + effective 18 July 1978 — formal Inter-American regional binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive human-rights framework + Inter-American Court of Human Rights IACtHR formal judicial-body framework, structurally distinctive globally as one of three modern regional human-rights frameworks with formal regional-court framework alongside ECHR + ACHPR-protocol-establishing-African-Court, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-human-rights frameworks which operate without formal regional-court framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Inter-American Article 17 family-rights framework (American Convention Article 17 framework on Rights of the Family — formal foundational family-rights framework underlying Inter-American parental-rights + child-protection case-law framework, distinct from ECHR Article 8 right to respect for private and family life framework operating through individual-rights-frame, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern regional human-rights binding-treaty framework operating formal family-rights-as-direct-right framework), as the central jurisdiction of the most distinctive modern Inter-American Court of Human Rights case-law framework on family-rights + child-rights (IACtHR Advisory Opinion OC-17/02 of 28 August 2002 on Juridical Condition and Human Rights of the Child + Atala Riffo and Daughters v. Chile 24 February 2012 + Fornerón and Daughter v. Argentina 27 April 2012 + multiple subsequent IACtHR cases applying Article 17 + 19 + 24 frameworks — formal IACtHR family-rights + child-rights case-law framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-developed modern regional human-rights framework on family-rights + child-rights), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal Inter-American American Convention Article 19 children's-rights framework (American Convention Article 19 framework on Rights of the Child — formal Inter-American framework predating UNCRC 1989 by 20 years, structurally distinctive globally as the earliest formal regional-binding-treaty children's-rights framework). The Inter-American Convention on Human Rights is binding upon 23 of 35 OAS member states that have ratified the Convention as of 2026 (excluding United States + Canada + most-of-Caribbean-CARICOM frameworks). The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 17 family-rights + Article 19 children's-rights frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework. The Inter-American Court of Human Rights is the apex judicial body framework; the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights is the formal monitoring body framework.
