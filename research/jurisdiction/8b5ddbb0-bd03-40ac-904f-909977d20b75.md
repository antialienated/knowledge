# Hokkaido Prefecture (北海道 / アイヌモシㇼ Aynu Mosir)

Hokkaido Prefecture (北海道 Hokkaido in Japanese, also encompassing the Ainu indigenous identity アイヌモシㇼ Aynu Mosir 'Land of the Ainu') is a Northern Japanese civil-law Japanese prefecture — structurally distinctive globally as the only Japanese prefecture with a formally-recognised indigenous people framework (Ainu Indigenous Peoples Promotion Act 2019 effective 24 May 2019 — first Japanese federal Act formally recognising the Ainu as 'indigenous people of Japan', representing a historic departure from prior Japanese state non-recognition of indigenous-people frameworks within Japan), as the largest Japanese prefecture by area (~83,424 km² — ~22% of Japanese land area, more than twice the area of the second-largest Japanese prefecture Iwate ~15,275 km²), as one of the most recently incorporated Japanese prefectures (Meiji-era colonisation framework 1869-1882 via the Kaitakushi 開拓使 Hokkaido Development Commission — distinct from rest-of-Honshu/Kyushu/Shikoku Japanese pre-Meiji feudal-domain framework), and as the central jurisdiction for the Russo-Japanese Northern Territories dispute (4 islands Kunashir/Kunashiri + Iturup/Etorofu + Shikotan + Habomai — Japanese 'Northern Territories' 北方領土 / Russian 'Southern Kurils' Южные Курилы — held by Soviet Union from 1945 and Russian Federation 1991-present, no Russo-Japanese WWII peace treaty concluded as of 2026). Hokkaido was historically the Ainu homeland Aynu Mosir prior to formal Japanese incorporation, was settled by Wajin (ethnic Japanese) populations under Matsumae Domain 1599-1869, was annexed by Meiji Japan via the Kaitakushi 開拓使 framework 1869-1882, established as Hokkaido Prefecture 1886, and subjected to systematic assimilation policies under the Hokkaido Former Aborigines Protection Act 1899 (repealed 1997 via the Ainu Cultural Promotion Act 1997, superseded 2019 via Ainu Indigenous Peoples Promotion Act). Hokkaido Prefecture comprises ~83,424 km² with a population of ~5.1 million. Family-law framework operates under the federal Japanese civil-law framework (Japanese Civil Code 1898 Family Law portion + Family Law Reform Act 2024 reintroducing joint parental authority post-divorce effective 2026) applied uniformly across Japanese prefectures. The Sapporo District Court is the primary trial court; the Sapporo High Court is the apex appellate court; final appellate jurisdiction lies with the Supreme Court of Japan. Hokkaido is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Japan is a Hague Convention 1980 signatory and party (acceded 24 January 2014 effective 1 April 2014).
