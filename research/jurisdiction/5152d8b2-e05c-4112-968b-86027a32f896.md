# Graubünden / Grisons / Grigioni / Grischun (Three Leagues)

Graubünden (German: Graubünden / French: Grisons / Italian: Grigioni / Romansh: Grischun) is an Eastern Swiss civil-law constituent canton of the Swiss Confederation — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern jurisdiction formally operating Romansh language in formal-administrative usage (Romansh recognised as official cantonal language alongside German + Italian via Cantonal Constitution 2003 Article 3, with Romansh recognised as Swiss Federal national language under Federal Constitution 1996 Article 4 + Federal Languages Act 2007 — Graubünden is the only modern jurisdiction globally where Romansh operates as official cantonal language, distinct from rest-of-Swiss-Confederation where Romansh has Federal-but-not-cantonal status), as the only trilingual Swiss canton (German ~74% + Romansh ~14% + Italian ~12% — distinct from rest-of-Swiss-cantons which operate monolingual or bilingual frameworks), as the largest Swiss canton by area (~7,105 km² — ~17% of Swiss land area, larger than any other Swiss canton including Bern + Valais), as the only modern Swiss canton with continuous pre-Confederation Three Leagues (Drei Bünde / Tres Lias) framework predating Swiss Confederation incorporation (Three Leagues 1395-1803 — Lega Caddea Grey League 1395 + Lega da la Chadè God's House League 1367 + Lega da las Dieschdretgiras Ten Jurisdictions League 1436, formally pre-Swiss-Confederation independent confederation framework, only joined Swiss Confederation via Napoleonic Act of Mediation 19 February 1803 — distinct from rest-of-Swiss-Confederation framework where most cantons joined 1291-1513), and as the central jurisdiction of pre-Confederation Rhaeto-Romanic continuous historical-cultural-political-linguistic framework (Romansh language descended from Vulgar Latin Rhaeto-Romanic Roman framework continuous since 15 BCE Roman conquest of Raetia — only modern Western European language with continuous Vulgar Latin lineage outside Romance-language-mainstream). Graubünden was historically the central Roman Raetia + the Three Leagues 1395-1803 + Napoleonic Helvetic Republic 1798-1803 + Swiss Confederation incorporation 19 February 1803 framework. Graubünden comprises ~7,105 km² with a population of ~200,000. Family-law framework operates under the federal Swiss civil-law framework (Swiss Civil Code 1907 + Swiss Civil Procedure Code 2008 + Swiss Same-Sex Marriage Reform 2022) applied uniformly across Swiss cantons. The Cantonal Court of Graubünden is the apex regional court; final appellate jurisdiction lies with the Swiss Federal Supreme Court. Graubünden is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Switzerland is a Hague Convention 1980 signatory and party (acceded 11 October 1983 effective 1 January 1984) — Graubünden is governed by Swiss Hague-Convention-1980 framework.
