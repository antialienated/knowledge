# Inter-American Convention on the International Return of Children 1989 (Montevideo Convention)

The Inter-American Convention on the International Return of Children 1989 (popularly the Montevideo Convention — formally Convención Interamericana sobre Restitución Internacional de Menores) is a Latin American supranational meta-framework cross-border-child-abduction-resolution instrument adopted by the Organization of American States (OAS) — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern regional cross-border-child-abduction-resolution framework operating alongside the Hague Convention 1980 framework (Inter-American Convention adopted 15 July 1989 by IV Inter-American Specialized Conference on Private International Law CIDIP-IV in Montevideo + effective 4 November 1994 — second international cross-border-child-abduction-resolution instrument in the world after Hague Convention 1980 + first regional cross-border-child-abduction-resolution instrument framework + formal Latin American-specific framework distinguishing from Hague Convention 1980 in formal Latin American-context-specific provisions, distinct from Hague Convention 1980 which operates universal multilateral framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Latin American post-1980 cross-border-child-abduction-resolution elaboration framework (Inter-American Convention 1989 adopted as Latin American regional response to Hague Convention 1980 framework + applicable to 14 Latin American + Caribbean state parties: Argentina + Bolivia + Brazil + Costa Rica + Ecuador + Guatemala + Mexico + Nicaragua + Paraguay + Peru + Uruguay + Venezuela + Suriname + Belize — 14 of 33 OAS member states have ratified the Convention as of 2026), as the only modern regional cross-border-child-abduction-resolution framework with formal Article 16(2) framework where Hague Convention 1980 and Inter-American Convention 1989 operate concurrently (Article 16(2) framework providing for hierarchical application of Inter-American Convention 1989 as priority framework where both Conventions are applicable between two Latin American state parties — distinct from Hague Convention 1980 multilateral framework), and as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern regional best-interests-of-the-child + parental-authority frameworks (Convention Article 1 cross-border-child-abduction-resolution framework + Article 2 child-under-16-years framework + Article 14 wrongful-removal-or-retention framework — formal Latin American-specific framework). The Inter-American Convention 1989 is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. The Inter-American Court of Human Rights is the apex judicial body for state-party violations.
