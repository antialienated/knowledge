# UN Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties 1969 (VCLT)

The UN Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties 1969 (VCLT / formally Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties) is a global supranational meta-framework treaty-law binding-treaty instrument adopted by the UN Conference of Plenipotentiaries — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on treaty-law framework + foundational framework for all subsequent modern international + regional + sub-national treaties (VCLT adopted 23 May 1969 by UN Conference of Plenipotentiaries on the Law of Treaties in Vienna + effective 27 January 1980 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive treaty-law framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-treaty frameworks which operate as substantive treaties without formal treaty-law framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-treaty-law-specific framework + foundational framework for all subsequent modern international + regional + sub-national treaties), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 31 + Article 32 interpretation-of-treaties framework (VCLT Article 31 framework providing 'A treaty shall be interpreted in good faith in accordance with the ordinary meaning to be given to the terms of the treaty in their context and in the light of its object and purpose' + Article 32 framework providing supplementary means of interpretation including preparatory work of the treaty + circumstances of its conclusion — formal foundational interpretation-of-treaties framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal interpretation-of-treaties framework, with substantial relevance to PA framework via formal interpretation-of-children's-rights-treaties framework + interpretation-of-Hague-Convention-1980-Article-13-b-grave-risk framework + interpretation-of-CEDAW-General-Recommendation-35 framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 27 internal-law-cannot-justify-failure-to-perform framework (VCLT Article 27 framework providing 'A party may not invoke the provisions of its internal law as justification for its failure to perform a treaty' — formal foundational pacta sunt servanda framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal pacta-sunt-servanda framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 53 jus-cogens-peremptory-norms framework (VCLT Article 53 framework providing 'A treaty is void if, at the time of its conclusion, it conflicts with a peremptory norm of general international law' — formal foundational jus-cogens-peremptory-norms framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal jus-cogens-peremptory-norms framework). The VCLT is binding upon 116 state parties as of 2026 — substantial state-party coverage framework + substantial customary-international-law framework operating beyond state-party framework via formal customary-international-law-application-to-non-state-parties framework. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 31 + 32 interpretation-of-treaties + Article 27 pacta-sunt-servanda + Article 53 jus-cogens-peremptory-norms frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal interpretation-of-children's-rights-treaties framework + interpretation-of-Hague-Convention-1980-Article-13-b-grave-risk framework. The UN Office of Legal Affairs is the formal monitoring framework body; the International Court of Justice ICJ is the apex judicial body for state-state disputes.
