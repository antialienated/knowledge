# UN International Convention for the Protection of All Persons from Enforced Disappearance (ICPED 2006)

The UN International Convention for the Protection of All Persons from Enforced Disappearance (ICPED) is a global supranational meta-framework enforced-disappearance binding-treaty instrument adopted by the UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on enforced-disappearance framework (ICPED adopted 20 December 2006 by UN General Assembly Resolution 61/177 + effective 23 December 2010 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive enforced-disappearance framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-human-rights frameworks which operate on broader general-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-enforced-disappearance-specific framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 25 children-victims-of-enforced-disappearance framework (ICPED Article 25 framework providing for state-party obligation to prevent + criminalise abduction of children whose parents are victims of enforced disappearance + abduction of children themselves subjected to enforced disappearance + falsification or destruction of children's identity documents + return of children with disappeared-parents framework — formal foundational children-victims-of-enforced-disappearance framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal children-victims-of-enforced-disappearance framework, with substantial relevance to PA framework via formal cross-border children-victims-of-parental-disappearance framework + formal children-with-disappeared-parents framework + 1976-1983 Argentinian-Dirty-War-stolen-children framework which catalysed ICPED development), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 24 victims-rights-to-the-truth framework (ICPED Article 24 framework providing 'Each victim has the right to know the truth regarding the circumstances of the enforced disappearance, the progress and results of the investigation and the fate of the disappeared person' — formal foundational victims-right-to-the-truth framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal victims-right-to-the-truth framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal Committee on Enforced Disappearances CED Committee framework with formal urgent-action procedure (CED Committee established under ICPED Article 26 + formal urgent-action procedure under ICPED Article 30 + formal individual communications framework under ICPED Article 31 + formal inter-state communications framework under ICPED Article 32 + formal visits framework under ICPED Article 33 — formal multi-procedure CED Committee framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal urgent-action procedure). The ICPED is binding upon 75 state parties as of 2026 — substantial state party + non-state-party framework reflecting growing but limited adoption framework. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 25 children-victims-of-enforced-disappearance + Article 24 victims-right-to-the-truth frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal cross-border children-victims-of-parental-disappearance framework + formal children-with-disappeared-parents framework. The Committee on Enforced Disappearances CED Committee is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies. The ICPED is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Parental-alienation recognition status: [object Object].
