# Hague Convention 1965 on Service of Documents (Service Convention)

The Hague Convention 1965 on Service of Documents (popularly the Service Convention / formally Convention of 15 November 1965 on the Service Abroad of Judicial and Extrajudicial Documents in Civil or Commercial Matters) is a global supranational meta-framework cross-border-service-of-documents binding-treaty instrument adopted by the Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH) — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on cross-border-service-of-documents framework (Service Convention adopted 15 November 1965 in The Hague + effective 10 February 1969 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive cross-border-service-of-documents framework via formal Central Authority + Request for Service framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-cross-border-service frameworks which operate on bilateral or letters-rogatory framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-cross-border-service-of-documents-via-Central-Authority-framework with substantial relevance to PA framework via formal cross-border family-law documents service framework + cross-border child-custody + divorce + adoption proceedings service framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 2 Central Authority framework (Service Convention Article 2 framework providing 'Each Contracting State shall designate a Central Authority which will undertake to receive requests for service coming from other Contracting States and to proceed in conformity with the provisions of Articles 3 to 6' — formal foundational Central Authority framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal Central Authority framework for cross-border-service-of-documents), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 5 service-methods framework (Service Convention Article 5 framework providing for service methods including by Central Authority + special method requested by applicant + service by such method 'as the law of the requested State prescribes for the service of documents in domestic actions upon persons who are within its territory' — formal foundational service-methods framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal e-Service Convention + e-APP Apostille framework (HCCH e-Service Convention + e-APP Apostille framework operational since 2007 — formal HCCH framework operating digital + electronic-service-of-documents framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal e-Service framework). The Service Convention is binding upon 81 state parties as of 2026 — substantial universal coverage framework. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 2 Central Authority + Article 5 service-methods + Article 15 default-judgment-non-appearance frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal cross-border family-law documents service framework + cross-border child-custody + divorce + adoption proceedings service framework. The Permanent Bureau of the Hague Conference on Private International Law is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level Central Authorities operate as administrative bodies.
