# Hague Convention 2007 on Child Maintenance + Hague Protocol 2007 (CETS-HCCH 2007)

The Hague Convention 2007 on Child Maintenance (formally Hague Convention on the International Recovery of Child Support and Other Forms of Family Maintenance) is a global supranational meta-framework cross-border-child-maintenance binding-treaty instrument adopted by the Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH) — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern global binding-treaty framework operating specifically on cross-border-child-maintenance framework (Hague Convention 2007 adopted 23 November 2007 in The Hague + effective 1 January 2013 — formal global binding-treaty framework establishing cooperation framework for cross-border-child-maintenance recovery + recognition + enforcement, distinct from Hague Convention 1980 + 1993 + 1996 frameworks which operate on cross-border-child-abduction-resolution + intercountry-adoption + parental-responsibility frameworks respectively, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern global binding-treaty framework operating on formal-cross-border-child-maintenance-specific framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal HCCH post-Hague-1996 child-maintenance elaboration framework (Hague Convention 2007 adopted as global comprehensive supplement to Hague Convention 1996 parental-responsibility framework + UN Convention on the Recovery Abroad of Maintenance 1956 framework — applicable to 49 state parties as of 2026, structurally distinctive globally as the most-developed modern global binding-treaty framework on cross-border-child-maintenance framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Hague Protocol 2007 on Maintenance applicable-law framework (Hague Protocol on the Law Applicable to Maintenance Obligations 2007 adopted 23 November 2007 in The Hague + effective 1 August 2013 — formal HCCH Protocol on applicable-law for maintenance obligations framework, distinct from Hague Convention 2007 which operates on cooperation framework, applicable to 33 state parties as of 2026, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern global binding-treaty framework operating on formal-applicable-law-for-maintenance-obligations-specific framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal Central Authority cooperation + Form Database + International Iberian Network IberRed framework (Convention Article 4 Central Authority designation framework + Article 5 Central Authority duties framework + Article 9 application to Central Authority framework + HCCH Country Profiles Database + International Iberian Network IberRed framework — formal HCCH-elaborated cross-border-child-maintenance-cooperation framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-children's-rights frameworks operating without formal Form Database + International Iberian Network framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern global binding-treaty framework operating formal Form Database + International Iberian Network framework). The Hague Convention 2007 is binding upon 49 state parties as of 2026 + the Hague Protocol 2007 is binding upon 33 state parties as of 2026 — substantial state party + non-state-party framework reflecting growing but limited adoption framework. The Convention + Protocol are silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal cross-border-child-maintenance + applicable-law frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal post-divorce + post-separation cross-border-child-maintenance framework. The Permanent Bureau of the Hague Conference on Private International Law is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level Central Authorities + national-level apex courts operate as administrative + judicial bodies. The Hague Convention 2007 + Hague Protocol 2007 are silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Parental-alienation recognition status: [object Object].
