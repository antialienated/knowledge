# European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR / CETS 005)

The European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR / formally Convention for the Protection of Human Rights and Fundamental Freedoms CETS 005) is a European supranational meta-framework human-rights binding-treaty instrument adopted by the Council of Europe — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern regional binding-treaty framework operating both formal human-rights binding-treaty framework + dedicated judicial-body framework with formal individual-applications jurisdiction (ECHR adopted 4 November 1950 + effective 3 September 1953 — formal European regional binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive human-rights framework + European Court of Human Rights ECtHR formal judicial-body framework with formal individual-applications jurisdiction, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-human-rights frameworks which operate without formal regional-court-individual-applications-jurisdiction framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern regional human-rights framework with formal regional-court-individual-applications-jurisdiction framework processing ~50,000+ applications per year), as the central jurisdiction of the most distinctive modern Strasbourg Article 8 positive-obligations doctrine framework (ECHR Article 8 framework on right to respect for private and family life — formal framework underlying European parental-rights + child-protection + parental-alienation case-law framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-developed modern regional human-rights framework on parental-rights + child-protection framework via Article 8 case-law framework including landmark cases Görgülü v. Germany 26 February 2004 + Maumousseau and Washington v. France 6 December 2007 + Neulinger and Shuruk v. Switzerland 6 July 2010 + Kupiec v. Poland 30 December 2010 + multiple subsequent ECtHR cases applying Article 8 positive-obligations doctrine framework to parental-alienation framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal European post-WWII human-rights elaboration framework (ECHR adopted as European regional foundational human-rights binding-treaty framework — applicable to all 46 Council of Europe member states as of 2026 with universal European adoption framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-widely-adopted regional human-rights binding-treaty framework with formal individual-applications-jurisdiction framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the European Court of Human Rights ECtHR framework with formal Grand Chamber + Chamber + Committee + Judge frameworks (ECtHR established 21 January 1959 under ECHR Article 19 framework + reformed via Protocol No. 11 1998 + Protocol No. 14 2010 + Protocol No. 15 2021 — formal European regional court framework with formal pilot-judgment + interim-measures + advisory-opinion frameworks, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-human-rights frameworks operating without formal regional-court framework). The ECHR is binding upon all 46 Council of Europe member states as of 2026 (excluding Russian Federation post-16 March 2022 expulsion + Belarus + Holy See never-joined frameworks). The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 8 framework operates substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via Article 8 positive-obligations doctrine framework. The European Court of Human Rights is the apex judicial body framework with formal individual-applications jurisdiction. The ECHR is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label.
