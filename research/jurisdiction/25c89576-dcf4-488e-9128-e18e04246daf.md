# Kurdistan Region of Iraq (هەرێمی کوردستان / إقليم كردستان)

The Kurdistan Region of Iraq (هەرێمی کوردستان Herêmî Kurdistan in Kurdish / إقليم كردستان Iqlīm Kurdistān in Arabic) is a Northern Iraqi mixed civil-law/Sharia-law constitutionally-recognised semi-autonomous region of the Federal Republic of Iraq — structurally distinctive globally as the only sub-state-level Kurdish jurisdiction with formal constitutional autonomy framework (Constitution of Iraq 2005 Article 117 formally recognising Kurdistan Region as constitutionally-recognised federal region — distinct from rest-of-Kurdish-population frameworks in Turkey, Syria, Iran where Kurdish-population sub-state-level autonomy is contested), as the only sub-state-level Kurdish jurisdiction with continuous de facto independence framework predating constitutional recognition (Kurdistan Regional Government KRG operations continuously since 1991 No-Fly Zone framework establishment 6 April 1991 following Gulf War 1991 — 14 years of de facto autonomy 1991-2005 prior to formal constitutional recognition), as the central jurisdiction of the contested 2017 Kurdistan independence referendum framework (Kurdistan Independence Referendum 25 September 2017 — 92.73% YES with 72.16% turnout, ~2.86 million YES votes out of ~3.31 million total — characterised by Iraqi Federal Government + Iran + Turkey as unilateral non-binding action), and as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern post-Saddam constitutional federal frameworks (post-2003 Iraq War + 2005 Iraqi Constitution federal framework + Iraqi Federal Supreme Court 2022 ruling rendering 2017 referendum unconstitutional). The Kurdistan Region historically operated under various Kurdish emirate frameworks (Soran Emirate 1816-1836 + Botan Emirate + Baban Emirate) + Ottoman vilayet framework + British Mandate Iraq 1920-1932 + Iraqi Kingdom 1932-1958 + Iraqi Republic 1958-2003 (including Anfal genocide 1986-1989 + Halabja chemical attack 16 March 1988 with ~5,000 deaths), achieved de facto autonomy 1991 following Gulf War No-Fly Zone establishment, established Kurdistan Regional Government 1992, achieved formal constitutional recognition via Iraqi Constitution 2005 Article 117, conducted 2017 Independence Referendum with 92.73% YES, and continues to operate as constitutionally-recognised federal region. Kurdistan Region of Iraq comprises ~46,861 km² (formal) / ~78,736 km² (with disputed territories) with a population of ~6.2 million. Family-law framework operates under a dual federal Iraqi civil-law framework (Iraqi Personal Status Law 188/1959) + Kurdistan-specific Family-Law Amendment 15/2008 framework. The Kurdistan Federal Court is the apex regional court; final appellate jurisdiction is contested between the Federal Supreme Court of Iraq + Kurdistan Federal Court. Kurdistan is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Iraq is a Hague Convention 1980 signatory but not a party — Kurdistan is governed by the Iraqi non-ratification status.
