# International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR 1966)

The International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) is a global supranational meta-framework civil + political rights binding-treaty instrument adopted by the UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as one of two universal multilateral binding-treaty translations of UDHR framework (ICCPR adopted 16 December 1966 by UN General Assembly Resolution 2200A (XXI) + effective 23 March 1976 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive civil + political rights framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-human-rights frameworks which operate on broader general-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as one of two universal multilateral binding-treaty translations of UDHR framework alongside ICESCR — formally constituting International Bill of Human Rights framework alongside UDHR + ICESCR), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 23 family-rights framework (ICCPR Article 23 framework on rights of the family — formal foundational family-rights framework establishing family as 'natural and fundamental group unit of society' entitled to formal state protection, structurally distinctive globally as one of the most-fundamental modern international family-rights framework provisions alongside UDHR Article 16 + American Convention Article 17 + Banjul Charter Article 18 frameworks), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 24 children's-rights framework (ICCPR Article 24 framework on rights of children — formal foundational children's-rights framework predating UNCRC 1989 by 23 years, structurally distinctive globally as one of the earliest formal universal binding-treaty children's-rights framework provisions), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal ICCPR Optional Protocol 1 individual communications framework (ICCPR Optional Protocol 1 adopted 16 December 1966 + effective 23 March 1976 — formal UN Human Rights Committee individual communications framework, applicable to 117 state parties as of 2026, structurally distinctive globally as one of the earliest formal universal binding-treaty individual-communications frameworks). The ICCPR is binding upon 174 state parties as of 2026 — substantial state party + non-state-party framework with significant non-ratification framework including China + Saudi Arabia + Singapore + Comoros + Cuba + Malaysia + others. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 17 privacy + Article 23 family-rights + Article 24 children's-rights frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework. The UN Human Rights Committee is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies. The ICCPR is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label.
