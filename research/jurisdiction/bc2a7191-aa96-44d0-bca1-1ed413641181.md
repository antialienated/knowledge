# UN Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (CERD 1965)

The UN International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (CERD) is a global supranational meta-framework racial-discrimination binding-treaty instrument adopted by the UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on racial-discrimination framework (CERD adopted 21 December 1965 by UN General Assembly Resolution 2106 (XX) + effective 4 January 1969 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive racial-discrimination framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-human-rights frameworks which operate on broader general-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-racial-discrimination-specific framework + first major UN human-rights treaty adopted after UDHR 1948 + Genocide Convention 1948 + CRPD ICCPR + ICESCR 1966 predecessor frameworks), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 1 racial-discrimination-definition framework (CERD Article 1 framework providing 'racial discrimination shall mean any distinction, exclusion, restriction or preference based on race, colour, descent, or national or ethnic origin which has the purpose or effect of nullifying or impairing the recognition, enjoyment or exercise, on an equal footing, of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural or any other field of public life' — formal foundational racial-discrimination-definition framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal racial-discrimination-definition framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 14 individual-communications-framework (CERD Article 14 framework providing 'A State Party may at any time declare that it recognizes the competence of the Committee to receive and consider communications from individuals or groups of individuals within its jurisdiction claiming to be victims of a violation by that State Party of any of the rights set forth in this Convention' — formal foundational individual-communications framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal optional individual-communications framework with 60 state parties as of 2026 accepting Article 14 framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal CERD Committee + State-State Inter-Communications Procedure framework via Qatar v Saudi Arabia 2018 + Qatar v United Arab Emirates 2018 + Palestine v Israel 2018 + Azerbaijan v Armenia 2022 + Armenia v Azerbaijan 2022 (CERD Committee + State-State Inter-Communications Procedure under CERD Articles 11-13 — formal foundational State-State Inter-Communications Procedure framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework with formal State-State Inter-Communications Procedure framework currently active). The CERD is binding upon 182 state parties as of 2026 — substantial universal coverage framework. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 1 racial-discrimination-definition + Article 5 equal-protection framework + Article 14 individual-communications frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal cross-border racial-discrimination-against-children-of-mixed-race-marriages framework. The CERD Committee is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies. The CERD is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label.
