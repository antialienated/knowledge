# Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH)

The Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH / formally Conférence de La Haye de droit international privé) is a global supranational meta-framework intergovernmental organisation operating private international law harmonisation framework — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern intergovernmental organisation operating private international law harmonisation framework with formal binding-treaty-elaboration framework (HCCH founded 12 September 1893 in The Hague by Tobias Asser — formal intergovernmental organisation operating private international law harmonisation framework, distinct from UN + other international organisations operating broader-scope frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern intergovernmental organisation operating private international law harmonisation framework with formal binding-treaty-elaboration framework + 89 state party + 1 EU bloc state party framework as of 2026), as the central jurisdiction of the formal HCCH Hague Conventions framework (HCCH has elaborated 41 Hague Conventions + 2 Hague Protocols + 1 Hague Principles + multiple Recommendations as of 2026 — formal HCCH Hague Conventions framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-developed modern intergovernmental organisation private international law harmonisation framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal HCCH Children-Family Hague Conventions framework (HCCH has elaborated multiple Children-Family Hague Conventions including Hague Convention 1956 on Child Maintenance + Hague Convention 1961 Adoption + Hague Convention 1965 Adoption + Hague Convention 1970 Recognition Divorces + Hague Convention 1973 Maintenance + Hague Convention 1980 Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction + Hague Convention 1993 Intercountry Adoption + Hague Convention 1996 Protection of Children + Hague Convention 2007 Child Maintenance + Hague Protocol 2007 Maintenance + others — formal HCCH Children-Family Hague Conventions framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-developed modern intergovernmental organisation Children-Family private international law harmonisation framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal HCCH Permanent Bureau institutional framework (HCCH Permanent Bureau formally established 1955 in The Hague — formal HCCH Secretariat institutional framework operating formal Council on General Affairs and Policy + Diplomatic Sessions + Special Commissions frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern intergovernmental organisation private international law harmonisation framework with formal Permanent Bureau institutional framework). The HCCH is binding upon 89 state parties + 1 EU bloc state party framework as of 2026 — substantial global coverage framework. The HCCH is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Hague Convention 1980 + Hague Convention 1996 + Hague Convention 1993 + Hague Convention 2007 frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework. The HCCH Permanent Bureau is the formal Secretariat institutional framework body; national-level apex courts + Central Authorities operate as administrative + judicial bodies.
