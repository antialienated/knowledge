# German-speaking Community of Belgium (Deutschsprachige Gemeinschaft Belgiens / DG / Eupen-Malmedy)

The German-speaking Community of Belgium (German: Deutschsprachige Gemeinschaft Belgiens / DG) is an Eastern Belgian civil-law constitutionally-recognised language community of the Kingdom of Belgium (one of three constitutionally-recognised language communities under Constitution of Belgium 1993 federal framework: Flemish Community + French Community + German-speaking Community) — structurally distinctive globally as the smallest constitutionally-recognised language community in the Belgian federal framework (~78,000 population — substantially smaller than Flemish Community ~6.7 million + French Community ~4.6 million, ~0.7% of Belgian population), as the only modern German-speaking jurisdiction formally annexed by a non-German state via the Treaty of Versailles 1919 (Eupen-Malmedy region transferred from German Empire to Belgium 28 June 1919 effective 10 January 1920 — only post-WWI German-Belgian border transfer framework, contested by post-WWI nationalist German frameworks 1920-1940), as a distinctive jurisdiction within Belgium for the German-speaking-Belgian-cultural-political-identity framework (Eupen + Malmedy + Sankt Vith German-speaking municipalities — formal German-language framework for legislative, executive, educational, judicial frameworks, distinct from rest-of-Belgium's Flemish + French language frameworks), and as the only Belgian language community with formal legislative + executive autonomy framework operating from a single integrated parliament + executive seat (Eupen — Parliament of the German-speaking Community + Government of the German-speaking Community + Ministry for the German-speaking Community framework). The German-speaking Community territory operated under the Prussian Rhine Province framework 1815-1919, was transferred from German Empire to Belgium via Treaty of Versailles 28 June 1919 effective 10 January 1920, suffered Belgian forced re-Germanisation 1940-1945 during Nazi German occupation, achieved formal community recognition under Constitution of Belgium 1970 community reform + 1980 community reform + 1993 federal framework. The German-speaking Community comprises 9 municipalities (Eupen + Malmedy + Sankt Vith + Amel + Burg-Reuland + Büllingen + Bütgenbach + Kelmis + Lontzen + Raeren — wait, Malmedy is in French-speaking Community despite German linguistic minority) — actual territorial framework is 9 municipalities in 2 districts (Eupen district + Sankt Vith district), comprises ~854 km² with a population of ~78,000. Family-law framework operates under the federal Belgian civil-law framework (Belgian Civil Code 1804 Family Law portion + Belgian Family-Law Reform 2024) applied uniformly across Belgian language communities. The Eupen Court of First Instance is the primary trial court; the Liège Court of Appeal is the apex regional appellate court; final appellate jurisdiction lies with the Court of Cassation of Belgium. The German-speaking Community is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Belgium is a Hague Convention 1980 signatory and party (acceded 11 January 1999 effective 1 May 1999) — German-speaking Community is governed by Belgian Hague-Convention-1980 framework.
