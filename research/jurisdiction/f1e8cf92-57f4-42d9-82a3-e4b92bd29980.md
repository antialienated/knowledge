# International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR 1966)

The International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR) is a global supranational meta-framework economic + social + cultural rights binding-treaty instrument adopted by the UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as one of two universal multilateral binding-treaty translations of UDHR framework alongside ICCPR (ICESCR adopted 16 December 1966 by UN General Assembly Resolution 2200A (XXI) + effective 3 January 1976 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive economic + social + cultural rights framework, distinct from ICCPR framework which operates on civil + political rights framework, structurally distinctive globally as one of two universal multilateral binding-treaty translations of UDHR framework alongside ICCPR — formally constituting International Bill of Human Rights framework alongside UDHR + ICCPR), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 10 family + maternity + children's-protection framework (ICESCR Article 10 framework on rights of the family + maternity + children's-protection — formal foundational family + maternity + children's-protection framework establishing 'the widest possible protection and assistance should be accorded to the family' + 'special protection should be accorded to mothers during a reasonable period before and after childbirth' + 'special measures of protection and assistance should be taken on behalf of all children and young persons', structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal combined family + maternity + children's-protection framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 13 right to education framework (ICESCR Article 13 framework on right to education — formal foundational right-to-education framework, structurally distinctive globally as one of the most-developed modern universal binding-treaty right-to-education framework provisions), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 12 right to health framework (ICESCR Article 12 framework on right to highest attainable standard of physical and mental health — formal foundational right-to-health framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-developed modern universal binding-treaty right-to-health framework provision). The ICESCR is binding upon 173 state parties as of 2026 — substantial state party + non-state-party framework with significant non-ratification framework including United States + Cuba + Comoros + Saint Lucia + South Africa + others. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 10 family + maternity + children's-protection framework intersects with parental-alienation framework. The UN Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies.
