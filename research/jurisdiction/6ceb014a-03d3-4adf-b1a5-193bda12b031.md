# Council of Europe Convention on Contact concerning Children 2003 (CETS 192)

The Council of Europe Convention on Contact concerning Children 2003 (formally Convention on Contact concerning Children CETS 192) is a European supranational meta-framework parental-contact-rights instrument adopted by the Council of Europe — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern international binding-treaty framework operating specifically on parental + family contact rights with children (Council of Europe Convention on Contact concerning Children adopted 15 May 2003 + effective 1 September 2005 — formal European regional binding-treaty framework establishing minimum-standard parental + family contact rights with children, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-children's-rights frameworks which operate on broader best-interests-of-the-child + general-children's-rights framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern international binding-treaty framework operating on formal-parental-and-family-contact-rights-specific framework), as the only modern international binding-treaty framework with formal contact-rights enforcement framework (Convention Article 14 trans-frontier contact framework + Article 15 trans-frontier contact safeguards framework + Article 17 cooperation between central authorities framework — formal contact-rights enforcement framework, distinct from Hague Convention 1980 + UNCRC + ACRWC + Inter-American Convention 1989 which operate broader frameworks), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Council of Europe post-Hague-1980 parental-contact-rights elaboration framework (Council of Europe Convention 2003 adopted as European regional supplement to Hague Convention 1980 framework — Article 22 framework providing for application alongside Hague Convention 1980 + applicable to 15 of 46 Council of Europe member states that have ratified the Convention as of 2026, with substantial remaining-states-not-ratified framework reflecting limited adoption of parental-contact-rights-specific framework), and as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern parental + family contact rights frameworks globally (Convention Article 2 contact-definition framework + Article 3 best-interests-of-the-child framework + Article 4 contact-rights of the child framework + Article 5 contact-rights of parents framework + Article 6 contact-rights of family-members framework — formal three-tier contact-rights framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-children's-rights frameworks operating on broader best-interests + parental-rights frameworks without formal three-tier contact-rights framework). The Convention is binding upon 15 Council of Europe member states that have ratified the Convention as of 2026: Albania + Andorra + Bosnia and Herzegovina + Croatia + Cyprus + Czech Republic + Iceland + Italy + Malta + Republic of Moldova + Romania + San Marino + Slovenia + Turkey + Ukraine. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal contact-rights framework is highly relevant. The European Court of Human Rights is the apex judicial body for state-party violations of related rights frameworks.
