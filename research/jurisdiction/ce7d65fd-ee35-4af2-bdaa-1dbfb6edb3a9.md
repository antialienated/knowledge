# Belém do Pará Convention (Inter-American Convention on the Prevention, Punishment and Eradication of Violence Against Women 1994)

The Belém do Pará Convention (formally Inter-American Convention on the Prevention, Punishment and Eradication of Violence Against Women / Convención Interamericana para Prevenir, Sancionar y Erradicar la Violencia contra la Mujer) is a Latin American supranational meta-framework violence-against-women binding-treaty instrument adopted by the Organization of American States (OAS) — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern regional binding-treaty framework operating specifically on violence-against-women framework predating Istanbul Convention 2011 framework (Belém do Pará Convention adopted 9 June 1994 by OAS XXIV General Assembly in Belém do Pará, Brazil + effective 5 March 1995 — formal Latin American regional binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive violence-against-women framework, distinct from Istanbul Convention 2011 framework adopted 17 years later, structurally distinctive globally as the first regional binding-treaty framework operating specifically on violence-against-women framework, predating Istanbul Convention 2011 framework by 17 years), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Inter-American post-CEDAW comprehensive-violence-against-women framework elaboration (Belém do Pará Convention adopted as Inter-American regional supplement to CEDAW 1979 framework — applicable to 32 of 34 OAS member states that have ratified the Convention as of 2026, with only United States + Cuba not ratifying framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most-widely-adopted regional binding-treaty framework on violence-against-women framework with near-universal Latin American + Caribbean adoption framework), as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern Inter-American Court of Human Rights case-law frameworks (Inter-American Court of Human Rights IACtHR González et al. (Cotton Field) v. Mexico 16 November 2009 + Veliz Franco et al. v. Guatemala 19 May 2014 + multiple subsequent IACtHR cases — formal IACtHR case-law framework applying Belém do Pará Convention framework to state-party violations, distinct from rest-of-modern-regional-women's-rights frameworks operating without formal regional-court-case-law framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the MESECVI monitoring framework (Inter-American Mechanism for the Follow-up of the Convention on the Prevention, Punishment and Eradication of Violence Against Women MESECVI established 26 October 2004 — formal dedicated monitoring-body framework with formal periodic-evaluation + Country Report framework, distinct from CEDAW Committee framework + GREVIO framework). The Belém do Pará Convention is binding upon 32 of 34 OAS member states that have ratified the Convention as of 2026 (excluding United States + Cuba). The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal violence-against-women framework intersects with parental-alienation framework. The MESECVI is the apex monitoring framework body; the Inter-American Court of Human Rights is the apex judicial body.
