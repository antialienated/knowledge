# International Court of Justice (ICJ / Statute of the ICJ 1945)

The International Court of Justice (ICJ / formally International Court of Justice + Statute of the ICJ adopted as Annex to UN Charter 1945) is a global supranational meta-framework UN-principal-judicial-body operating via Statute adopted by the UN Conference of Plenipotentiaries — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral judicial body framework operating both formal state-state-contentious-jurisdiction framework + formal advisory-opinion-jurisdiction framework (ICJ Statute adopted 26 June 1945 + effective 24 October 1945 alongside UN Charter — formal universal multilateral judicial body framework establishing comprehensive ICJ framework + formal complementary framework to UN Charter, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-courts which operate as regional or specialized frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral judicial body framework operating both formal state-state-contentious-jurisdiction framework + formal advisory-opinion-jurisdiction framework + formal successor framework to Permanent Court of International Justice PCIJ 1922-1946 framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Statute Article 38 sources-of-international-law framework (ICJ Statute Article 38 framework providing 'The Court, whose function is to decide in accordance with international law such disputes as are submitted to it, shall apply: a. international conventions; b. international custom; c. the general principles of law recognized by civilized nations; d. judicial decisions and the teachings of the most highly qualified publicists of the various nations, as subsidiary means for the determination of rules of law' — formal foundational sources-of-international-law framework underlying all subsequent modern international + regional + sub-national international-law frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral judicial body framework operating formal sources-of-international-law framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 36(2) optional-clause-acceptance framework (ICJ Statute Article 36(2) framework providing 'States parties to the present Statute may at any time declare that they recognize as compulsory ipso facto and without special agreement, in relation to any other state accepting the same obligation, the jurisdiction of the Court in all legal disputes' — formal foundational optional-clause-acceptance framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral judicial body framework operating formal optional-clause-acceptance framework with 73 state parties as of 2026), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal LaGrand 2001 + Avena 2004 + Genocide Convention 2007 + South Africa v Israel Genocide Application 2024 ICJ case-law framework on family-rights + children's-rights frameworks (ICJ Germany v United States of America LaGrand Judgment 27 June 2001 + Mexico v United States of America Avena and Other Mexican Nationals Judgment 31 March 2004 + Bosnia and Herzegovina v Serbia and Montenegro Genocide Application Judgment 26 February 2007 + South Africa v Israel Genocide Application order 26 January 2024 — formal foundational ICJ case-law framework with substantial relevance to PA framework via formal cross-border consular-protection-of-children-of-divorced-parents framework + cross-border-genocide-against-children framework + cross-border-deportation-of-children framework). The ICJ Statute is binding upon 193 state parties as of 2026 — universal coverage framework via UN Charter framework. The Statute is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 38 sources-of-international-law + Article 36(2) optional-clause-acceptance + formal ICJ case-law on cross-border family-rights + children's-rights frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal interpretation-of-international-law-on-children's-rights frame
