# Statute of the Council of Europe 1949 (Treaty of London)

The Statute of the Council of Europe (Treaty of London) is a regional supranational meta-framework foundational-Council-of-Europe-treaty framework adopted by the 10 founding European states in London — structurally distinctive globally as the oldest modern European supranational binding-treaty framework operating on pan-European framework with formal Article 3 democracy-rule-of-law-human-rights admission framework (Statute of the Council of Europe signed 5 May 1949 in London + effective 3 August 1949 — formal foundational Council of Europe treaty framework establishing comprehensive Council of Europe organisation framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-regional-European-treaty frameworks which operate as subsequent European treaties, structurally distinctive globally as the oldest modern European supranational binding-treaty framework + parent organisation of European Convention on Human Rights ECHR 1950 + parent organisation of European Court of Human Rights ECtHR 1959 framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 3 Admission Framework (Council of Europe Statute Article 3 framework providing 'Every member of the Council of Europe must accept the principles of the rule of law and of the enjoyment by all persons within its jurisdiction of human rights and fundamental freedoms' — formal foundational democracy-rule-of-law-human-rights admission framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern regional supranational binding-treaty framework operating formal Article 3 admission framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal European Convention on Human Rights ECHR framework (ECHR adopted 4 November 1950 in Rome Italy + effective 3 September 1953 + ECtHR established 21 January 1959 + Protocol 11 reorganisation 1998 + Protocol 14 2010 + Protocol 16 advisory opinions 2018 — formal foundational European-regional-human-rights framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern regional supranational binding-treaty framework operating formal single-court permanent-court European-regional-human-rights framework with individual-petition framework as default since Protocol 11), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Council of Europe Convention on Contact concerning Children 2003 framework (CETS 192 adopted 15 May 2003 in Strasbourg + effective 1 September 2005 providing formal Europe-regional cross-border-contact-with-children framework — formal foundational Europe-regional cross-border-contact-with-children framework with PA-intersection framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern regional supranational binding-treaty framework operating formal cross-border-contact-with-children framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal Council of Europe Russia Expulsion 2022 framework (Committee of Ministers Resolution CM/Res(2022)2 of 16 March 2022 expelling Russia from Council of Europe over invasion of Ukraine — formal foundational Council of Europe expulsion framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern regional supranational binding-treaty framework operating formal expulsion-over-aggression framework). The Council of Europe Statute is binding upon 46 Council of Europe member states as of 2026 — comprehensive pan-European coverage framework (Russia expelled 2022). The Statute is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal ECHR + Council of Europe Convention on Contact concerning Children 2003 frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal Europe-regional cross-border-children's-rights framework. The Committee of Ministers + Parliamentary Assembly + European Court of Human Rights ECtHR + Commissioner for Human Rights are the formal Council of Europe bodies; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies. The Council of Europe Statute is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Parental-alienation recognition status: [object Object].
