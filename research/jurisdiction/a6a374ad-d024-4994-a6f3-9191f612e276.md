# Gagauzia / Gagauz Yeri / Гагаузия (Autonomous Territorial Unit of Gagauzia)

Gagauzia (Gagauz: Gagauz Yeri / Russian: Гагаузия Gagauziya / Romanian: Găgăuzia) is a Southern Moldovan civil-law Autonomous Territorial Unit (UTA) of the Republic of Moldova — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern Turkic-speaking Orthodox-Christian-majority sub-national jurisdiction (~95% Turkic-speaking Gagauz population + ~95% Eastern Orthodox Christian framework — distinct from rest-of-Turkic-speaking-world which operates predominantly Islamic-majority frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only sub-national jurisdiction where Turkic-language speakers operate Orthodox Christian framework), as the only modern post-Soviet sub-national territory established via formal Moldovan-Gagauzian negotiated autonomy agreement (Law on the Special Legal Status of Gagauzia 1994 effective 23 December 1994 — formal Moldovan-Gagauzian-negotiated autonomy framework following Gagauz Republic 1990-1995 secessionist movement, distinct from rest-of-post-Soviet sub-national frameworks established via formal independence-declaration or armed-conflict frameworks), as the central jurisdiction of the most distinctive modern Moldovan sub-national constitutional right of external self-determination (Law on the Special Legal Status of Gagauzia Article 1(4) — formal constitutional right of Gagauzia to declare independence in the event of Moldovan loss of independence, distinct from rest-of-modern-sub-national frameworks globally where formal right of external self-determination is not constitutionally guaranteed), and as the central jurisdiction of the most contested modern Moldovan-Russian sub-national pro-Russian framework (Gagauz Yeri 2014 referendum 6 February 2014 — 98.4% vote for closer ties with Eurasian Customs Union + 97.2% against EU integration, with 70.4% turnout, characterised by Moldovan government as unconstitutional + characterised by Gagauz government as legitimate consultative-referendum framework). Gagauzia historically operated under various Ottoman-Crimean-Tatar-Wallachian frameworks 14th-18th centuries + Russian Empire 1812-1917 + Romanian Bessarabia 1918-1940 + Soviet Moldavian SSR 1940-1991 + Gagauz Republic 1990-1995 + Autonomous Territorial Unit of Gagauzia 1995+. Gagauzia comprises ~1,832 km² with a population of ~130,000 (predominantly Gagauz Turkic-speakers). Family-law framework operates under the federal Moldovan civil-law framework (Moldovan Family Code 2000 + Moldovan Civil Code 2002) applied uniformly across Moldovan sub-national territories. The Court of Comrat is the primary trial court; the Court of Appeal Cahul is the apex regional appellate court; final appellate jurisdiction lies with the Supreme Court of Moldova. Gagauzia is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Moldova is a Hague Convention 1980 signatory and party (acceded 10 April 1998 effective 1 July 1998) — Gagauzia is governed by Moldovan Hague-Convention-1980 framework.
