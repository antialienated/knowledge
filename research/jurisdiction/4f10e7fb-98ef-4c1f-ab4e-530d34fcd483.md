# Hague Convention 1996 on the Protection of Children

The Hague Convention 1996 on the Protection of Children (formally Convention on Jurisdiction, Applicable Law, Recognition, Enforcement and Co-operation in Respect of Parental Responsibility and Measures for the Protection of Children) is a global supranational meta-framework parental-responsibility + child-protection-measures instrument adopted by the Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH) — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern global binding-treaty framework operating jurisdiction + applicable-law + recognition + enforcement + cooperation frameworks for parental-responsibility + child-protection-measures (Hague Convention 1996 adopted 19 October 1996 + effective 1 January 2002 — formal global binding-treaty framework establishing comprehensive jurisdiction + applicable-law + recognition + enforcement + cooperation framework, distinct from Hague Convention 1980 which operates specifically on cross-border-child-abduction-resolution framework, structurally distinctive globally as the most comprehensive modern global binding-treaty framework on parental-responsibility + child-protection-measures), as the central jurisdiction of the formal HCCH post-Hague-1980 comprehensive-parental-responsibility framework elaboration (Hague Convention 1996 adopted as global comprehensive supplement to Hague Convention 1980 framework — applicable to 56 state parties as of 2026, with substantial state parties from Council of Europe + Latin America + Asia frameworks, distinct from Hague Convention 1980 which has 103 state parties as of 2026, with limited adoption framework reflecting comprehensive-framework adoption-difficulty), as the only modern global binding-treaty framework operating formal five-pillar framework (Convention Article 1 jurisdiction framework + Article 15 applicable-law framework + Article 23 recognition framework + Article 26 enforcement framework + Article 29 cooperation framework — formal five-pillar global comprehensive parental-responsibility framework), and as the central jurisdiction of one of the most distinctive modern global parental-responsibility frameworks globally (Convention Article 5 habitual-residence framework + Article 10 child-protection-measures framework + Article 16 parental-responsibility framework + Article 23 recognition-of-foreign-decisions framework — formal global comprehensive parental-responsibility framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-children's-rights frameworks operating on broader best-interests + general-children's-rights frameworks without formal five-pillar framework). The Hague Convention 1996 is binding upon 56 state parties as of 2026 including substantial Council of Europe + Latin American + Asian state parties + EU bloc Brussels IIa Regulation framework operating as functional equivalent. The Convention is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal parental-responsibility framework is highly relevant. The Permanent Bureau of the Hague Conference on Private International Law is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies.
