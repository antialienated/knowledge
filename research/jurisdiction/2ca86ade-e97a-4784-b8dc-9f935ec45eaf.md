# Ceuta (Ciudad Autónoma de Ceuta / سَبْتَة)

Ceuta (Spanish: Ciudad Autónoma de Ceuta / Arabic: سَبْتَة Sabta) is a North African civil-law Autonomous City of the Kingdom of Spain — structurally distinctive globally as one of two modern European Union sub-national territories located on the African mainland (alongside Melilla — distinct from rest-of-EU-sub-national-territories located in Europe or non-African overseas territories), as one of two Spanish Autonomous Cities (Ciudades Autónomas — Ceuta + Melilla, constitutionally distinct sui generis territorial framework under Constitution of Spain 1978 Article 144(b) + Statute of Autonomy of Ceuta 1995, distinct from rest-of-Spanish-territorial framework operating 17 Autonomous Communities + 50 Provinces), as one of the longest-continuously-Spanish sub-national territories (Ceuta under Portuguese control 1415-1640 + Spanish control 1640+ via the Iberian Union dissolution Treaty of Lisbon 1668 — formal Spanish framework 1668-present 358+ years, longer than most modern Spanish sub-national territories), as the central jurisdiction of one of the most contested modern Spanish-Moroccan sovereignty frameworks (Morocco maintains formal sovereignty claim over Ceuta since Moroccan independence 1956 — distinct from rest-of-Spanish-sub-national territorial framework where sovereignty is uncontested), and as a distinctive multi-religious Spanish sub-national jurisdiction (~47% Christian + ~45% Muslim + ~5% Jewish + ~2% Hindu + others — distinctive multi-religious framework, distinct from rest-of-Spanish-sub-national-territorial Christian-majority framework). Ceuta historically operated under Phoenician 7th century BCE + Carthaginian + Roman + Visigothic + Byzantine + Arab Berber Caliphate framework 711 CE + Portuguese 1415-1640 + Spanish 1640+ via the Iberian Union dissolution + Treaty of Lisbon 1668 + Spanish municipality framework 1668-1995 + Autonomous City of Ceuta 1995+. Ceuta comprises ~19 km² with a population of ~83,000. Family-law framework operates under the federal Spanish civil-law framework (Spanish Civil Code 1889 Family Law portion) applied uniformly across Spanish sub-national territories. The Court of First Instance and Instruction of Ceuta is the primary trial court; the Superior Court of Justice of Andalusia, Ceuta and Melilla is the apex regional appellate court; final appellate jurisdiction lies with the Supreme Court of Spain. Ceuta is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Spain is a Hague Convention 1980 signatory and party (acceded 16 June 1987 effective 1 September 1987) — Ceuta is governed by Spanish Hague-Convention-1980 framework.
