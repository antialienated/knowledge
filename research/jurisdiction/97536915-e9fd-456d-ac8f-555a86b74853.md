# EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR 2016/679)

The EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR / formally EU Regulation 2016/679 of 27 April 2016 on the Protection of Natural Persons with Regard to the Processing of Personal Data) is a European Union supranational meta-framework data-protection binding-regulation instrument adopted by the European Parliament + Council — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework operating EU bloc binding data-protection framework with formal extraterritorial-applicability framework (GDPR adopted 27 April 2016 + effective 25 May 2018 — formal EU bloc binding data-protection framework establishing comprehensive data-protection framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-data-protection frameworks which operate as national-level frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework operating EU bloc binding data-protection framework with formal extraterritorial-applicability framework under Article 3 Territorial Scope applicable to non-EU-bloc-data-controllers processing EU-data-subjects' personal data), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 8 children-consent framework (GDPR Article 8 framework providing 'the processing of personal data of a child shall be lawful where the child is at least 16 years old. Where the child is below the age of 16 years, such processing shall be lawful only if and to the extent that consent is given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility over the child' — formal foundational children-consent framework with formal-parental-responsibility-consent framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework operating formal children-consent framework with parental-responsibility-consent framework, with substantial relevance to PA framework via formal cross-border-data-processing-of-children-of-divorced-parents framework + formal disputes-between-parents-over-children-consent framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 6 lawful-basis-for-processing framework (GDPR Article 6 framework providing 6 lawful bases for processing including consent + contract + legal obligation + vital interests + public task + legitimate interests — formal foundational lawful-basis-for-processing framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework operating formal six-pillar-lawful-basis-for-processing framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal European Data Protection Board EDPB monitoring framework (EDPB established under GDPR Article 68 — formal EU bloc data-protection monitoring framework with formal binding-decision framework via Article 65 consistency mechanism, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern supranational meta-framework operating formal EU bloc data-protection monitoring framework with formal binding-decision framework). The GDPR is binding upon all 27 EU member states + 3 EEA member states (Iceland + Liechtenstein + Norway) + applicable to non-EU-bloc-data-controllers processing EU-data-subjects' personal data via Article 3 extraterritorial-applicability framework — universal de facto coverage framework via Article 3. The Regulation is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 8 children-consent + Article 6 lawful-basis-for-processing + Article 12-23 data-subject-rights frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal cross-border-data-processing-of-children-of-divorced-parents framework + formal disputes-between-parents-over-children-consent framework. The European Data Protection Board EDPB is the apex monitoring framework body; the Court of Justice of the European Union CJEU is the apex judicial body framework; national-level Data Protection Authorities DPAs operate as administrative bodies. The GDPR is silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label. Parental-alienation recognition status: [object Object].
