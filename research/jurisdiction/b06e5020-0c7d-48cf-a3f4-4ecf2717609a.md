# UN Refugee Convention 1951 + 1967 Protocol (UN 1951 Convention + 1967 Protocol Relating to the Status of Refugees)

The UN Refugee Convention 1951 + 1967 Protocol Relating to the Status of Refugees (formally Convention relating to the Status of Refugees + Protocol Relating to the Status of Refugees) is a global supranational meta-framework refugee-protection binding-treaty duo adopted by the UN Conference of Plenipotentiaries + UN General Assembly — structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal multilateral binding-treaty framework operating specifically on refugee-protection framework (UN Refugee Convention adopted 28 July 1951 by UN Conference of Plenipotentiaries on the Status of Refugees and Stateless Persons in Geneva + effective 22 April 1954 + 1967 Protocol adopted 31 January 1967 by UN General Assembly + effective 4 October 1967 — formal universal multilateral binding-treaty duo establishing comprehensive refugee-protection framework, distinct from rest-of-modern-international-human-rights frameworks which operate on broader general-rights frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating on formal-refugee-protection-specific framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 1A(2) refugee-definition framework (Convention Article 1A(2) framework providing 'owing to well-founded fear of being persecuted for reasons of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political opinion, is outside the country of his nationality and is unable or, owing to such fear, is unwilling to avail himself of the protection of that country' — formal foundational refugee-definition framework with formal 'particular social group' framework underlying gender-based persecution + LGBTI+ persecution + cross-border family violence persecution-claim frameworks, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal refugee-definition framework), as the central jurisdiction of the formal Article 33 non-refoulement framework (Convention Article 33 framework providing 'No Contracting State shall expel or return a refugee in any manner whatsoever to the frontiers of territories where his life or freedom would be threatened on account of his race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political opinion' — formal foundational non-refoulement framework, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern universal binding-treaty framework operating formal non-refoulement framework), and as the central jurisdiction of the formal UNHCR Mandate framework (UN High Commissioner for Refugees UNHCR established by UN General Assembly Resolution 428 (V) of 14 December 1950 — formal UNHCR Mandate framework operating identification + protection + assistance + durable-solutions framework for refugees, structurally distinctive globally as the only modern UN agency framework with formal Refugee Mandate). The 1951 Convention is binding upon 146 state parties as of 2026 + the 1967 Protocol is binding upon 147 state parties as of 2026 — substantial state party + non-state-party framework reflecting widespread global adoption framework. The Conventions are silent on 'parental alienation' as a statutory label, though formal Article 1A(2) refugee-definition + Article 33 non-refoulement + Article 22 access to elementary education + Article 24 labour legislation frameworks operate substantial intersection with parental-alienation framework via formal cross-border family-violence refugee-claim framework + formal cross-border children-of-refugee-parents framework + formal cross-border separation-of-refugee-parents-from-children framework. The UN High Commissioner for Refugees UNHCR is the apex monitoring framework body; national-level apex courts operate as judicial bodies.
