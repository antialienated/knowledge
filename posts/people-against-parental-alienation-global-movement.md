## Why the Movement Is Growing Now

Several converging factors explain the explosive growth:

### 1. Post-COVID Court Backlogs Exposed the System

During the pandemic, millions of parents were denied court access while alienation accelerated. When courts reopened, the backlog revealed **systemic failures** that had been hidden for years. Parents who might have suffered in silence discovered online communities of thousands facing identical patterns.

### 2. Social Media Broke the Silence

TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube gave alienated parents a platform. Hashtags like **#ParentalAlienation**, **#AlienatedParent**, and **#FactsOverFeelings** have accumulated over **2 billion views** combined. For the first time, the emotional reality of alienation became visible to the general public.

### 3. Academic Legitimacy Reached Critical Mass

With over **1,000 peer-reviewed publications** on parental alienation, the scientific community can no longer be dismissed. The inclusion of alienation-related codes in ICD-11 (2022) provided international clinical recognition.

### 4. Legislative Breakthroughs Proved Change Is Possible

Brazil criminalised alienation in 2010. Italy strengthened penalties in 2023. Belgium's astreinte system provides financial enforcement. Each victory emboldened advocates in other countries.

## Parental Alienation Awareness Day (April 25)

Established in 2006, **April 25** is globally recognised as Parental Alienation Awareness Day (PAAD). What started as a small US initiative has become a worldwide event:

- **Marches and rallies** in major cities (London, Brussels, Sydney, Toronto, Washington DC)

- **Educational events** — webinars, conferences, and panel discussions hosted by legal and mental health professionals

## Legislative Victories Around the World

### 🇧🇷 Brazil — Law 12.318/2010

The world's first anti-alienation law. Defines alienation, lists specific behaviours, and empowers judges to:
- Issue warnings
- Order counselling
- Modify custody arrangements
- Fine or imprison repeat offenders

### 🇧🇪 Belgium — Astreinte System

Belgian courts can impose **daily financial penalties** (astreinte) for non-compliance with custody orders. Typical amounts: €50-€500 per day of denied contact.

### 🇮🇹 Italy — Criminal Code Art. 388

Italy treats custody order violations as a criminal offence. Courts can impose fines and imprisonment for persistent alienation.

### 🇲🇽 Mexico — Federal Civil Code Reform (2024)

Mexico added alienation as a ground for custody modification in federal family law.

### 🇺🇸 United States — State-Level Progress

Multiple states have introduced alienation-awareness legislation:
- **Florida** — Presumption of equal time-sharing
- **Texas** — Senate Bill 1237 addresses alienating behaviours
- **Connecticut** — Kayden's Law-inspired measures
