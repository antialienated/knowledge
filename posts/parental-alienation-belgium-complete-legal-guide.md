# Parental Alienation in Belgium: The Complete Legal Guide for 2026

Belgium has one of the most developed legal frameworks in Europe for addressing parental alienation.

Yet most parents — and many lawyers — do not fully understand the tools available to them.

This guide provides a comprehensive overview of Belgian law as it applies to parental alienation, including civil remedies, criminal prosecution, enforcement mechanisms, and the role of the European Court of Human Rights.

---

## The Legal Foundation: Belgian Civil Code

### Article 374 — Joint Parental Authority

This is the cornerstone of Belgian family law regarding parental rights after separation.

**Key provisions:**
- Both parents exercise parental authority jointly, regardless of whether they are married or separated
- Both parents have equal rights to make decisions about the child's education, health, religion, and welfare
- Both parents have the right to be informed about all aspects of the child's life
- The child has the right to maintain personal relationships and direct contact with both parents

When one parent systematically excludes the other from decision-making or information, they are violating Article 374.

### Article 371/1 — The Child's Right to Both Parents

This article explicitly establishes that **the child has the right to maintain personal relationships and direct contact with each parent**.

This is not merely a parental right — it is framed as the child's right. This distinction is legally significant because it means the court's primary consideration is the child's welfare, not the parents' competing claims.

### Article 374/2 — Exclusive Parental Authority

When joint authority fails due to one parent's obstruction, the family court may award **exclusive parental authority** to one parent.

This is one of Belgium's most powerful remedies for alienation: the court can transfer primary residence and decision-making authority to the targeted parent if the alienating parent's behavior is sufficiently documented.

## Enforcement: Article 387ter

Article 387ter provides the enforcement mechanism for custody orders.

**Available measures:**

1. **Astreintes (Financial Penalties):** The court can impose fines for each violation of a custody order. These are cumulative — €500 per missed exchange can add up quickly.

2. **Forced Execution:** In extreme cases, the court can authorize the use of force (police assistance) to execute custody transfers.

3. **Residence Transfer:** The court can transfer the child's primary residence from the alienating parent to the targeted parent.

4. **Modified Custody Arrangements:** The court can restructure the custody arrangement to reduce the alienating parent's control.

> 💡 **Pro Insight:** Belgian judges are generally reluctant to use forced execution or residence transfer as a first resort. They typically start with astreintes and escalate. This means documenting a **pattern of non-compliance** is essential — a single violation rarely triggers the strongest remedies.

## Criminal Law: When Alienation Becomes a Crime

### Article 432 — Non-Representation of a Child

Refusing to hand over a child to the parent entitled to custody is a criminal offense in Belgium.

**Penalties:**
- Imprisonment of 8 days to 1 year
- Fines

This applies when a parent refuses to comply with a court-ordered custody arrangement. A police report should be filed for each violation.

### Article 443 — Defamation and Slander

If the alienating parent makes false statements about the targeted parent — to the child, to institutions, or publicly — this may constitute defamation under Belgian criminal law.

**Elements required:**
- A false statement of fact (not opinion)
- Made to a third party
- That damages the targeted parent's reputation

> ⚠️ **Common Mistake:** Many parents skip filing police reports because they believe "nothing will happen." While criminal prosecution for Article 432 violations is not guaranteed, each police report creates an official record that can be referenced in family court proceedings.

## The Role of Belgian Family Courts

### The Tribunal de la Famille

Since the 2014 reform, Belgium has dedicated family courts (tribunaux de la famille) that handle all family matters in a single jurisdiction.

**Key advantages:**
- One judge follows the case from start to finish
- All family-related matters (custody, support, enforcement) are handled together
- The court has access to the full history of the family dispute

### Expert Evaluations

The family court can order:
- **Social investigations** by the Service de Protection Judiciaire (SPJ)
- **Psychological evaluations** of parents and children
- **Mediation** (required attempt before litigation in many cases)

### The Role of the Procureur du Roi

The public prosecutor (Procureur du Roi) can intervene in family cases where the child's welfare is at risk. In severe alienation cases, the prosecutor can:
- Request urgent protective measures
- Initiate criminal proceedings for non-representation
- Refer the case to youth protection services

## European Court of Human Rights: Article 8

Belgium, as a signatory to the European Convention on Human Rights, is bound by Article 8 — the right to respect for private and family life.

The ECHR has established that:
- States have a **positive obligation** to take effective measures to reunite parent and child
- Failure to enforce custody orders can constitute a violation of Article 8
- The passage of time cannot be used as justification for inaction — delays caused by the state's own failure to act cannot be held against the targeted parent

**Key ECHR cases relevant to Belgian practitioners:**

1. **Diamante Ferrara v. Italy (2023):** Violation found where the state failed to take adequate measures to enforce custody over a period of years.

2. **Piazzi v. Italy (2018):** The court held that the state's passive response to custody violations constituted a violation of the father's Article 8 rights.

3. **Neulinger and Shuruk v. Switzerland (2010):** International relocation case where the court balanced the child's best interests against the right to family life.

4. **Sommerfeld v. Germany (2003):** The court emphasized that domestic courts must consider the views of all parties, including the child, but that a child's expressed wishes do not override the obligation to investigate whether those wishes are genuine.

## Practical Strategy for Belgian Cases

### Step 1: Document Everything Under Article 374

Create a detailed record showing:
- Information you were excluded from (school, medical, activities)
- Decisions made without your consent
- Communication you attempted that was blocked or ignored

### Step 2: File for Enforcement Under Article 387ter

Do not wait for the alienation to become severe. File for enforcement at the first pattern of non-compliance.

Request:
- Astreintes for each future violation
- A court-ordered communication protocol
- Clear, specific terms in the custody order that leave no room for interpretation

### Step 3: File Police Reports Under Article 432

For every instance of non-representation, file a police report. Even if criminal prosecution does not follow, the reports create an official record.

### Step 4: Request Expert Evaluation

Ask the court to appoint a qualified expert to evaluate the family dynamic. Specifically request an expert with training in parental alienation.

### Step 5: Consider ECHR Application

If Belgian courts fail to act effectively over a prolonged period, you may have grounds to file an application with the European Court of Human Rights under Article 8.

**Requirements:**
- All domestic remedies must be exhausted
- The application must be filed within 4 months of the final domestic decision (changed from 6 months in 2022)
