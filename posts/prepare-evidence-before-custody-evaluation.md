A custody evaluation can shape the outcome of your case more than any hearing.

Most parents walk in with a bag of printouts and a lot of emotion. Evaluators remember the parent who walked in with a timeline.

This guide covers how to organise evidence so an evaluator can verify it quickly.

It is for any parent with an upcoming psychological or social-services custody evaluation.

## What the Evaluator Is Actually Looking For

Evaluators are not judging who is the better person. They assess each parent's capacity, the child's needs, and whether each parent supports the child's relationship with the other.

That last point matters most in alienation cases. Your evidence should show you support the relationship even when the other parent does not.

## Step-by-Step: Building Your Evaluation File

### Step 1: Start With a One-Page Timeline
Key dates only: separation, orders, major incidents, changes in contact. One page. Every line references a source document.

### Step 2: Group Evidence by Question
Organise under Compliance, Involvement and Interference. Not by date, not by file type.

### Step 3: Show Involvement First
School emails, medical appointments, activities attended. Proof you parent comes before proof they obstruct.

### Step 4: Select, Don't Dump
Choose the ten strongest items per category. Keep the rest available on request.

### Step 5: Link Every Claim to a Source
Every statement in your summary should point to a dated original: email, message export, police report or court document.

> ⚠️ Common Mistake
> Handing over 400 pages. Evaluators skim volume. They read structure.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Criticising the other parent's character instead of documenting behaviour
- Coaching the child before interviews
- Bringing evidence you cannot trace back to an original file
- Arguing with the evaluator's questions

## Advanced Strategies

> 🧠 Mental Model
> Write for a tired reader with forty minutes. If a point cannot be verified in thirty seconds, it will not land.

Show patterns with counts: "Contact happened on 11 of 26 scheduled weekends." Numbers survive cross-examination better than adjectives.

> 💡 Pro Insight
> Include one example where you facilitated contact for the other parent. It demonstrates the co-parenting capacity evaluators are trained to look for.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I give the evaluator everything?**
No. Give a curated file and offer the full archive on request.

**Can I include text messages?**
Yes. Use full exports with timestamps rather than cropped screenshots.

**Should I mention parental alienation by name?**
Describe the behaviours and let the documented pattern speak. Labels invite debate; dated facts do not.

**How far back should the timeline go?**
From separation, or from the current order if the history is long.

**What if the other parent lies in their interview?**
You cannot rebut in the room. Your source-linked file is what corrects the record.

## Final Takeaway

An evaluation rewards clarity. One timeline, three categories, every claim sourced.

Facts over feelings. Let the evidence speak.
